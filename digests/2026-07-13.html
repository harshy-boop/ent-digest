Double-Blind RCT Multicentre (7 sites) n=75

Endolymphatic Duct Blockage for Ménière's Disease: A Double-Blind, Randomised Controlled Trial

Schenck AA, van de Berg R, Peters TTA, Smulders YE … Smulders YE (senior, Dept. Otorhinolaryngology & Head and Neck Surgery, Leiden University Medical Centre, the Netherlands)

BACKGROUND

Ménière's disease treatment remains contentious. EDB (endolymphatic duct blockage) was proposed as superior to ESD (endolymphatic sac decompression) for intractable disease, but no double-blind trial had been conducted.

METHODS

Double-blind multi-center RCT at 2 university and 5 non-university Dutch hospitals. 75 patients with definite unilateral Ménière's refractory to ≥2 corticosteroid injections were randomized intraoperatively to EDB (n=39) or ESD (n=36); both patients and investigators blinded for 12-month follow-up. Primary outcome: vertigo attack freedom (no attacks in preceding 6 months at 12-month visit).

RESULTS

59% EDB vs 67% ESD achieved the primary outcome (OR 0.72, 95% CI 0.28–1.84; p=0.65). No between-group differences in QOL, dizziness, tinnitus, or audiometry. Three serious AEs (one EDB, two ESD); no deaths. Higher baseline treatment expectations were the strongest predictor of success in both arms.

LIMITATIONS

Moderate sample (n=75); neither arm included a sham-surgery control, so absolute surgical benefit above natural history is unknown. The expectation effect may mask lack of true therapeutic benefit for either procedure.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

EDB offers no advantage over ESD for refractory Ménière's disease — do not preferentially adopt EDB. The strong expectation-outcome correlation raises the possibility that much perceived benefit from both procedures is non-specific; future trials should incorporate a sham arm.

Why score 5: Double-blind, multi-center RCT in Lancet Regional — one of the few blinded surgical trials in otology. Negative primary outcome (EDB no better than ESD) is itself practice-defining and overturns enthusiasm for the newer technique.

Prospective Cohort Population-Based n=80 cCMV

Dried Blood Spot Testing in Confirmed Congenital Cytomegalovirus Discovered by Expanded Targeted Testing

Kfoury P, Reddy M, Blackstad M, McVicar SB … McVicar SB (senior, Dept. Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, University of Utah School of Medicine)

BACKGROUND

Congenital CMV (cCMV) is the most common non-genetic cause of sensorineural hearing loss. Whether dried blood spot (DBS) universal screening or expanded targeted testing is preferable for identifying affected newborns is unresolved.

METHODS

Population-based cohort of 80 cCMV-positive newborns from 26 Utah birth hospitals identified by expanded targeted testing (2019–2024). Stored neonatal DBS samples underwent retrospective PCR (UL83 or IE gene) at the University of Minnesota, blinded to clinical status.

RESULTS

DBS PCR sensitivity 86.3% (95% CI 77%–92%) overall; 92.7% (95% CI 80.6%–97.5%) for moderate-to-severe symptomatic cCMV; and 78.1% (95% CI 61.3%–89.0%) for mild symptomatic disease. Specificity 99.5%; PPV 100%; NPV 94.3%.

LIMITATIONS

Sample restricted to confirmed cCMV cases — cannot model universal screening performance directly. Wide CIs due to n=80. Asymptomatic infants (at highest risk of delayed-onset hearing loss) are underrepresented.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Universal DBS screening would miss ~14% of cCMV cases overall — and ~22% of mild symptomatic cases most at risk for delayed-onset SNHL. Expanded targeted testing captures more. Pediatric otolaryngologists should note: a negative DBS does not exclude cCMV in a child with hearing loss.

Why score 4: Prospective cohort in JAMA Otolaryngology addressing a policy-relevant clinical question — DBS screening adequacy — with direct implications for hearing loss prevention programs in cCMV.

RCT Economic Evaluation 17 UK Centers

Cost-Effectiveness of Immediate Septoplasty Versus Medical Management With the Option for Delayed Septoplasty: A Multicentre RCT

Homer T, Carrie S, Fouweather T, Teare D … Teare D (senior, Population Health Sciences Institute, Newcastle University, UK)

BACKGROUND

Whether to proceed with immediate septoplasty or trial 6 months of medical management in patients with symptomatic deviated septum lacks high-quality economic evidence.

METHODS

Economic evaluation alongside a 17-center, open-label UK RCT. Adults with NOSE score >30 randomized to immediate septoplasty (within 12 weeks) or 6-month medical management (nasal steroid + saline spray) with option for delayed surgery. Primary economic outcome: incremental cost per QALY (SF-36); extrapolated to 24 months.

RESULTS

Immediate septoplasty was more costly (+£1,193) and more effective (+0.044 QALYs) at 12 months. Base-case ICER: £27,114/QALY; micro-costing ICER: £16,682/QALY. At 24 months, immediate septoplasty had a 99% probability of cost-effectiveness at the UK £20,000/QALY threshold.

LIMITATIONS

Open-label design; UK NHS costs may not generalize to US payors. Recruited 2018–2019. The 'delayed surgery' crossover option may underestimate the true cost difference between arms.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For patients with moderate-to-severe nasal obstruction from septal deviation, immediate septoplasty is highly cost-effective over 24 months — the strongest economic evidence yet for a surgery-first approach. Useful when insurers mandate medical management trials before approving septoplasty.

Why score 4: Multi-center RCT economic analysis across 17 UK otolaryngology sites directly addresses the 'operate now vs. trial medical therapy' rhinology decision. 99% probability cost-effective at 24 months supports surgery-first approach.

Delphi Consensus Multidisciplinary n=45 Experts

Consensus Guidelines for Staging and Surveillance Imaging in Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Kassamali B, Schoenfeld JD, Sethi R, Bakst RL … Bakst RL (senior, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School)

BACKGROUND

CSCC staging and surveillance imaging practices vary widely due to broad NCCN guidance. No multidisciplinary consensus existed for when and how to image high-risk cutaneous SCC.

METHODS

45-expert Delphi panel (dermatology 33%, rad oncology 20%, radiology 20%, medical oncology 16%, surgery 7%, otolaryngology 4%) completed 3 iterative survey rounds (Jan–Jun 2025). Consensus ≥80%; near-consensus 70–79%.

RESULTS

Imaging recommended for CSCC with ≥15% estimated metastasis risk: bone invasion, invasion beyond subcutaneous fat, large-caliber perineural invasion, tumor ≥4 cm, or poorly-differentiated tumors ≥2 cm with LVI or PNI. CT preferred for nodal staging (84%) and surveillance (78%). Surveillance for at least 2–3 years.

LIMITATIONS

Expert consensus, not prospective outcome data. Risk thresholds not prospectively validated. ENT representation was 4% of panel — head/neck-specific concerns may be underweighted.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Head and neck surgeons co-managing high-risk CSCC now have clearer imaging criteria: stage with CT when tumor ≥4 cm, deeply invasive, or poorly differentiated with additional risk factors; maintain CT surveillance for 2–3 years. Standardizes a long-variable practice.

Why score 4: Multidisciplinary Delphi in JAMA Dermatology fills a genuine practice gap. ENT/H&N surgeons who co-manage high-risk CSCC gain clear, consensus-backed criteria for CT staging and surveillance timing.

Consensus Guidelines International Multidisciplinary 305 Studies Reviewed

Global Consensus on the Management of Primary Localized Chordoma

Radaelli S, Frezza AM, Fossati P, Baldi GG … Baldi GG (senior, Global Chordoma Consensus Group)

BACKGROUND

Chordoma is a rare malignant bone tumor affecting skull base, mobile spine, and sacrum, with 40–60% metastatic spread over the disease course. The previous consensus was published in 2015; advances in pathology, imaging, and RT modalities necessitate an update.

METHODS

June 2025 Milan meeting of the Global Chordoma Consensus Group, including experts across skull base surgery, spinal surgery, radiation oncology, pathology, radiology, and patient advocates. Literature review of 305 articles; levels of evidence and grades of recommendation assigned per anatomical site.

RESULTS

Core principle: treatment at experienced multidisciplinary centers is essential. Maximal safe surgery followed by high-dose, highly conformal radiotherapy (proton, carbon-ion, or photon SBRT) remains central. Site-specific guidance for skull base, mobile spine, and sacrum; updated recommendations for systemic therapy, long-term risk-adapted surveillance, and supportive/palliative care.

LIMITATIONS

Recommendations largely expert opinion and retrospective data due to disease rarity. No Level 1 RCT evidence available. Specialized center requirement limits generalizability to community practice.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

ENT/skull base surgeons encountering clival or anterior skull base chordomas should use these 2025 guidelines: refer to experienced multidisciplinary centers, target maximal safe resection, ensure high-dose conformal RT (proton preferred for skull base). The 2015 consensus is now superseded.

Why score 4: JAMA Oncology-published international consensus supersedes the 2015 guidelines for chordoma — a skull base tumor ENT surgeons encounter at the clivus. Updated guidance on surgical approach, conformal RT, and systemic options directly affects subspecialty practice.
