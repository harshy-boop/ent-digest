Early Endoscopic Repair of Traumatic Skull Base Dural Injuries: Challenging the Dogma of Conservative Management.

Grayson JW, Bald MP, McLeod L et al. … Woodworth BA (senior, Department of Otolaryngology Head & Neck Surgery, University of Alabama at Birmi)

BACKGROUND

Conservative management of traumatic skull base dural injury has historically been favored, a paradigm established when craniotomy was the only operative option. The rise of endoscopic skull base surgery warrants a reassessment of this approach, particularly given the known risk of intracranial infection with prolonged CSF exposure.

METHODS

Retrospective review of 385 consecutive patients with traumatic skull base injury and radiographic or clinical evidence of dural disruption at UAB. All patients with pneumocephalus or CSF leak underwent early endoscopic repair rather than observation. A subset of 102 patients had prior conservative management at referring institutions.

RESULTS

Primary endoscopic repair success was achieved in 97.4% (375/385), rising to 99.0% (381/385) after endoscopic revision. Among 102 patients managed conservatively before referral, 34 (33.3%) developed meningitis or intracerebral infection. Ethmoid, frontal, and sphenoid sinuses were most commonly involved.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center retrospective design at a high-volume referral center limits generalizability. Lack of randomization means the conservative cohort had selection differences. Surgeon experience at a dedicated skull base program may not reflect outcomes at community hospitals.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Pneumocephalus on imaging should be recognized as a marker of dural disruption even without overt CSF rhinorrhea, and early endoscopic repair should be strongly considered rather than observation. One-in-three conservatively managed patients in this series developed intracranial infection — a sobering benchmark against which to weigh the 97% success rate of endoscopic repair.

Why score 4: Large single-institution retrospective (n=385) from UAB's premier skull base program (Woodworth). 97.4% primary endoscopic repair success; 33.3% of conservatively managed patients developed meningitis/intracerebral infection. Compellingly challenges the 'observe-and-wait' dogma with robust data. IFAR flagship + large N justify score 4.

Pathologic Response After Neoadjuvant Chemoimmunotherapy in Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma.

Gomez EA, Kraft DO, Elkersh Y et al. … Roof SA (senior, Department of Otolaryngology, Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York,)

BACKGROUND

Locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) carries poor outcomes, and neoadjuvant immunotherapy (NAI) with single-agent PD-1 inhibitors has shown only modest pathologic response rates (~10–20%). Combining neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (NACI) follows a conceptually similar approach to what proved effective in other solid tumors.

METHODS

Retrospective cohort of 86 adults with resectable HNSCC at Icahn/Mount Sinai (July 2024–March 2026) who received ≥1 cycle of NAI (n=28) or NACI (n=58) before definitive surgery. Propensity score adjustment was applied to account for baseline differences. Pathologic response was graded as pCR (no viable tumor), MPR (≤10% viable), or partial.

RESULTS

NACI achieved pCR or MPR ('deep pathologic response') in 67% (39/58) vs 3.6% (1/28) with NAI alone. In propensity score-adjusted analysis, OR for deep response was 29.1 (95% CI 6.7–274.7). Overall treatment tolerance was good with few adverse event–related surgical delays.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center retrospective design, modest overall N, and inability to control for all unmeasured confounders. Pathologic response is a surrogate endpoint — prospective data on survival impact are not yet available. Case-mix between NAI and NACI groups likely differed despite propensity adjustment.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For surgeons and oncologists managing resectable HNSCC outside of clinical trials, these data suggest adding chemotherapy to neoadjuvant immunotherapy is associated with substantially higher pathologic response rates. While awaiting randomized trial data, NACI appears to be the more active regimen for patients who are candidates for neoadjuvant treatment.

Why score 4: Single-center propensity score–adjusted retrospective cohort published in JAMA Otolaryngology (flagship). Dramatic effect size: NACI achieved deep pathologic response in 67% vs 3.6% for immunotherapy alone (OR 29.1). Despite retrospective design and modest N, the magnitude of the finding is practice-influencing for off-trial neoadjuvant therapy decisions in HNSCC.

Association of Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Analogues and Laryngeal Symptoms in Obese Adults.

Shah P, Kayekjian D, Nguyen SA et al. … O'Rourke AK (senior, Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Medical University of South )

BACKGROUND

GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide, etc.) are prescribed to tens of millions of patients globally for obesity and diabetes. Upper GI motility effects and delayed gastric emptying are established; laryngeal effects have been undercharacterized despite the proximity of GLP-1 receptors to the laryngopharyngeal mucosa.

METHODS

TriNetX US Collaborative Network retrospective cohort study of adults with obesity on GLP-1RA (n=617,296) from 2016–2025 vs controls (n=3,503,102). Patients with head/neck neoplasms, radiation, autoimmune disease, or prior airway disorders were excluded. Outcomes assessed at 1, 3, and 6 months post-initiation in propensity score-matched cohorts.

RESULTS

GLP-1RA use was associated with higher odds of any laryngeal manifestation at 6 months (OR 1.19, 95% CI 1.16–1.21). Specific elevations: cough (OR 1.46), foreign body sensation (OR 1.41), voice and resonance disorders (OR 1.19). Exenatide, liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, and lixisenatide all showed significant elevation; tirzepatide did not.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective database design limits causal inference; ICD-10 coding may capture overlapping diagnoses. Absolute risk differences were small despite statistical significance. Confounding by indication (obese patients more prone to laryngopharyngeal reflux) cannot be fully excluded. Tirzepatide's null result may reflect shorter exposure time in the database.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Patients presenting with new cough, globus sensation, or hoarseness should be asked about GLP-1RA use. With millions on these agents, even a small per-patient risk translates to a large volume of referrals. Laryngologists should include GLP-1RA on their medication review, and prescribers should counsel patients about potential laryngeal side effects.

Why score 4: Landmark-scale TriNetX retrospective cohort (617,296 GLP-1RA users vs 3.5M controls) with propensity score matching from MUSC. GLP-1RA associated with 19% higher odds of laryngeal symptoms; cough, foreign body sensation, and voice/resonance disorders specifically elevated. Clinically urgent given the explosive growth of GLP-1RA/GIP prescribing; laryngologists need to recognize this drug class as a cause. Laryngoscope flagship, very large N.

Baseline EPOS 2020 eligibility and real-world outcomes of dupilumab in CRSwNP: a longitudinal cohort study.

Campion NJ, Petsiou DP, Fally F et al. … Schneider S (senior, Vienna Airway Lab, Department of Otorhinolaryngology, Medical University of Vien)

BACKGROUND

Dupilumab is FDA-approved for CRSwNP but costs >$35,000/year. EPOS2020 introduced indication criteria to guide biologic therapy selection, yet their real-world predictive validity had not been prospectively validated in a large cohort.

METHODS

224 CRSwNP patients treated with dupilumab at the Medical University of Vienna (Nov 2019–May 2025). Patients were stratified as EPOS2020-eligible (n=76) or ineligible (n=101). Outcomes included Total Polyp Score, SNOT-22, olfaction (UPSIT), and serum biomarkers. Response defined by MCID thresholds and EUFOREA criteria.

RESULTS

EPOS2020-eligible patients showed greater improvements in polyp score, SNOT-22, and olfaction. NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease (NSAID-ERD/AERD) independently predicted adequate response. Higher baseline serum IgE was associated with faster improvement trajectories. 'Adequate EUFOREA response' rates did not differ between eligibility groups — suggesting EPOS criteria may be conservative.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective stratification and single-center design. EPOS2020 criteria could be fully assessed in only 177 of 224 patients. The relatively short 6-year observation window does not capture durability data at decade timescales.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

EPOS2020 criteria provide a defensible framework for biologic allocation, but NSAID-ERD/AERD is a clinically intuitive, readily ascertained predictor that should prompt earlier or stronger consideration of dupilumab. Patients with high baseline IgE may respond more rapidly. Active type 2 biomarkers may outperform categorical eligibility criteria for real-world response prediction.

Why score 4: Well-characterized real-world prospective cohort (n=224) from Vienna Airway Lab in Rhinology (flagship). Validates EPOS2020 indication criteria for dupilumab as clinically predictive; NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease is a strong independent predictor of response. Directly applicable to the high-cost biologic prescribing decision in CRSwNP.

Improving Functional Oral Intake in Head and Neck Cancer Survivors with Chronic Radiation-Associated Dysphagia - The Multicenter Randomized Controlled HIT-CRAD Trial.

Massonet H, Goeleven A, Van Den Steen L et al. … Van Nuffelen G (senior, Department of Translational Neurosciences, University of Antwerp, Antwerp, Belgi)

BACKGROUND

Chronic radiation-associated dysphagia (CRAD) affects a substantial proportion of head and neck cancer survivors after radiotherapy and is associated with aspiration, malnutrition, and reduced quality of life. The optimal rehabilitation approach — strength training alone vs. combined strength and skill training — has not been rigorously compared.

METHODS

54 HNC survivors with CRAD randomized to 3 groups (n=18 each): (1) high-volume tongue/chin tuck/expiratory strength training only; (2) same regimen for 4 weeks followed by 4 weeks McNeill Dysphagia Therapy Program skill training (sham HD-tDCS); (3) combined training with real HD-tDCS. All performed exercises 4×/week for 8 weeks.

RESULTS

All groups showed significant improvements in functional oral intake (p<0.001), swallowing function (p<0.05), and muscle strength (p<0.001). Combined strength+skill training (groups 2+3 vs. group 1) had significantly greater odds of improved oral intake (OR 13.47, 95% CI 5.95–30.49). HD-tDCS added no benefit beyond sham (groups 2 vs. 3).

LIMITATIONS

Small N per arm (n=18 each), single therapist training protocol may limit generalizability. Eight-week program duration; durability of gains beyond treatment was not assessed. HD-tDCS blinding may have been imperfect in some subjects.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For HNC survivors with chronic radiation dysphagia, combining strength and skill-based swallowing therapy (McNeill Dysphagia Program) substantially outperforms strength-only exercise programs. HD-tDCS stimulation over the swallowing motor cortex does not add benefit. Clinicians should refer to programs combining both strength and functional task training rather than strength exercises alone.

Why score 4: Multicenter RCT (n=54) evaluating swallowing rehabilitation regimens for chronic radiation-associated dysphagia in HNC survivors. Direct head-to-head comparison of strength vs. combined strength+skill training; OR 13.47 for combined approach. While the Dysphagia journal is not flagship, the randomized design in a highly prevalent and debilitating condition earns score 4.

Clinical Longitudinal Evaluation After Regimen Stop (CLEAR) of Dupilumab in Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps (CRSwNP).

Retrospective cohort of 46 CRSwNP patients who stopped dupilumab after ≥2 years of excellent disease control (SNOT-22 ~10, NPS ~0.2). Median time to symptom worsening was only 7.6 weeks. By 12 months, 87% had reinitiated dupilumab due to recurrence; only 6 patients remained off therapy (with ongoing objective worsening). Retreatment produced rapid re-response. These data support the concept of dupilumab as a disease-controlling rather than disease-modifying therapy; individual dose tapering appears preferable to full cessation for most patients.

SEER analysis of 955 SDC cases from 2000–2022. Age-adjusted incidence increased from 0.158 to 1.18 per 100,000 (10.38% average annual percent change, p<0.001), accelerating after 2012. Drug-Induced Sleep Endoscopy in Infants With Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Current Evidence and Knowledge Gaps.

PRISMA-ScR scoping review synthesizing 8 observational studies (151 infants, 2013–2024) on DISE in infants with OSA. DISE revealed multilevel obstruction in most cases, predominantly supraglottic (laryngomalacia-equivalent on DISE) and retrolingual. DISE appears feasible and clinically valuable in infants but current evidence is limited by small, heterogeneous series; prospective standardized studies are needed.

3 Network Meta-Analysis
Efficacy and safety of oral treatments for allergic rhinitis in children: A network meta-analysis.

Network meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=906) evaluating oral antihistamines (OAH) and leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRA) for allergic rhinitis in children <12 years. OAH were superior to placebo in improving TNSS (MD −1.60). Combined OAH+LTRA did not demonstrate additional benefit over OAH alone (MD −0.15, CI −1.18 to 0.88). No significant differences in adverse event rates. GRADE certainty was moderate to low. For pediatric allergic rhinitis, oral antihistamines appear adequate as monotherapy; adding montelukast does not improve symptom scores, supporting simplified prescribing in children.

Watchful Waiting Compared With Immediate Antibiotics for Urban Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Children With Uncomplicated Acute Otitis Media (WATCH): A Non-Inferiority Randomised Controlled Trial.

Multicenter non-inferiority RCT (8 Aboriginal Medical Services, Australia) of 263 children aged 1.5–16 with uncomplicated AOM randomized to watchful waiting (n=134) or immediate antibiotics (n=129). Day-7 clinical resolution was 53.8% (watchful waiting) vs 60.2% (antibiotics), difference −6.4 percentage points (90% CI −17.4 to 4.6) — non-inferiority was NOT established. Aboriginal children, who face higher rates of chronic otitis media and hearing loss, may not be the right population for watchful waiting protocols validated in low-risk European cohorts.

ACDF Impact on Aerodigestive Function and Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Scoping Review.

Scoping review in the Laryngoscope examining how anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) affects aerodigestive function and OSA. Post-ACDF complications including pharyngeal edema, dysphagia, and muscle weakness are established; this review examines emerging evidence that ACDF may both worsen and, in some cases, improve OSA depending on the spinal level and mechanism.
