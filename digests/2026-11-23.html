Zopapogene Imadenovec-drba Treatment for Adults With Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis: A Review

Norberg SM, Misono S, Allen CT … Allen CT (senior, National Cancer Institute, NIH)

BACKGROUND

Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) is caused by HPV 6/11 and characterized by recurrent airway growths requiring repeated surgical debulking; until recently no FDA-approved non-surgical treatment existed. Zopapogene imadenovec-drba is an HPV-targeted gene therapy that has now received FDA approval and been designated first-line treatment by the RRP Foundation.

METHODS

Narrative review synthesizing clinical trial data, the FDA approval package, and practical implementation considerations—patient eligibility, administration logistics, monitoring protocols, adverse effect management, and transition frameworks for patients currently managed with surveillance or repeat debulking.

RESULTS

Zopapogene imadenovec-drba is recommended as first-line treatment in eligible adults with RRP per the RRP Foundation Position Statement. The review provides specific guidance on patient selection criteria, dosing schedules derived from the clinical trials, and how to integrate the therapy alongside ongoing or future surgical management.

LIMITATIONS

Narrative review format introduces selection bias and is not a systematic evidence synthesis; long-term real-world effectiveness, durability of response, and optimal retreatment strategies are still emerging from post-approval experience; clinical trial populations may not fully reflect the diversity of real-world RRP patients.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Otolaryngologists managing adult RRP should immediately familiarize themselves with zopapogene imadenovec-drba protocols; the standard of care has changed and all eligible patients currently undergoing surveillance or repeat surgical debulking should be evaluated for first-line gene therapy at their next clinic encounter.

Why score 4: JAMA Otolaryngology narrative review addressing practical implementation of the first FDA-approved therapy for RRP; though narrative review methodology limits methodology score, the practice-changing nature of a new FDA-approved first-line treatment for a previously treatment-refractory disease makes this essential reading for any clinician managing RRP.

Prognostic Value of Adverse Pathological Features in Early-Stage Oral Cavity Squamous Cell Carcinoma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

de Castro LCM, Silva WPS, Macedo MSR, de Castro RB … Filho WA (senior, Federal University of Ceara Medical School, Fortaleza, Brazil)

BACKGROUND

Adverse pathological features (APFs)—depth of invasion (DOI), perineural invasion (PNI), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and worst pattern of invasion (WPOI)—are increasingly incorporated into staging and management decisions for oral cavity SCC. Their individual and combined prognostic value in early-stage (T1-T2N0M0) disease had not been comprehensively synthesized.

METHODS

Systematic search of MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, and trial registries for cohort studies of surgically treated T1-T2N0M0 oral cavity SCC reporting DOI, PNI, LVI, or WPOI in relation to nodal metastasis, locoregional recurrence, disease-specific survival, or overall survival. Study quality was assessed and meta-analyses performed for each adverse feature.

RESULTS

All four APFs showed statistically significant associations with occult nodal metastasis, locoregional recurrence, and overall survival. DOI (using established thresholds) and PNI emerged as the strongest predictors of nodal spread and recurrence. Combined APF burden conferred substantially worse prognosis than any single feature alone.

LIMITATIONS

All included studies are retrospective; heterogeneity in DOI thresholds, PNI definition, and WPOI scoring systems across centers limits direct pooling. Adjuvant treatment regimens varied across studies, potentially confounding survival analyses. High-quality prospective data remain sparse for this stage group.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

In T1-T2N0 oral cavity SCC, the presence of PNI, LVI, elevated DOI beyond the established thresholds, or WPOI-5 on final pathology should reliably trigger consideration of adjuvant therapy and/or more aggressive neck management; pathology reports should explicitly document all four APFs and their combination for every early-stage OCSCC resection.

Why score 4: Systematic review and meta-analysis in Head & Neck synthesizing the independent prognostic impact of DOI, PNI, LVI, and WPOI in T1-T2N0M0 oral cavity SCC across multiple datasets; directly informs the most common surgical decision points (elective neck dissection, adjuvant therapy) in early-stage oral cancer and fills a gap in synthesized guidance for this stage.

Multicenter CT-Based Deep Learning for Preoperative Nodal Risk Stratification in Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma

Xu S, Yan X, Su Y, Qi J … Du Y (senior, Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology)

BACKGROUND

Accurate preoperative prediction of central compartment lymph node metastasis (LNM) in papillary thyroid carcinoma guides decisions about prophylactic central neck dissection. Current imaging (ultrasound, CT) detects only clinically apparent nodes; subclinical metastasis is missed in up to 40-60% of cN0 patients. AI-based models using CT imaging could improve preoperative stratification.

METHODS

ThyLNT, a Transformer-based 2.5D deep learning model using preoperative CT imaging, was developed in a training/internal validation cohort from Tongji Hospital and externally validated in five independent cohorts from different hospitals (1,560 patients total). Multi-omics analysis was performed to identify biological phenotypes correlated with LNM risk.

RESULTS

ThyLNT demonstrated superior performance for LNM prediction compared to conventional CT interpretation and previously published models across all five external validation cohorts, with consistent AUC above conventional benchmarks. The model identified biologically distinct phenotypes with differing LNM risks, adding mechanistic interpretability beyond imaging alone.

LIMITATIONS

All cohorts are retrospective; model requires preoperative CT (not universally obtained for every PTC patient); computational infrastructure is needed for real-world deployment. Performance in low-volume or non-academic centers has not been tested. Long-term surgical outcome data with model-guided versus standard decision-making are not reported.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

High-volume thyroid surgery programs with routine preoperative CT capability should evaluate ThyLNT-type tools as a supplement to ultrasound for preoperative LNM risk stratification; in institutions where CT is obtained, applying a validated deep learning model may identify cN0 patients who are most likely to benefit from prophylactic central neck dissection.

Why score 4: Large multicenter retrospective study in the flagship thyroid journal developing and externally validating ThyLNT, a Transformer-based CT deep learning model for preoperative LNM prediction in PTC across 1,560 patients and 6 hospitals with 5 external validation cohorts; strong external validation and multi-omics integration make this the most credible CT-AI tool for thyroid nodal staging published to date.

Prophylactic Central Neck Dissection in Clinically Node-Negative, Low- to Intermediate-Risk Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma

Authors (multicenter collaboration)

BACKGROUND

Whether to perform prophylactic central neck dissection (pCND) alongside total thyroidectomy in clinically node-negative (cN0) papillary thyroid carcinoma remains one of thyroid surgery's most debated decisions, balancing completeness of staging and potential locoregional control against substantially increased risk of hypoparathyroidism and recurrent laryngeal nerve injury.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis incorporating available randomized controlled trial data alongside observational studies evaluating pCND versus thyroidectomy alone in cN0 PTC stratified as low to intermediate risk. Primary outcomes included locoregional recurrence, biochemical evidence of persistent disease, temporary and permanent hypoparathyroidism, and RLN injury.

RESULTS

pCND was associated with improved locoregional control in higher-risk cN0 patients but with statistically significant increases in temporary hypoparathyroidism. No significant benefit was demonstrated for low-risk microcarcinoma. The meta-analysis identified subgroups most likely to benefit including larger primaries, multifocal disease, and cases with features of extrathyroidal extension.

LIMITATIONS

Heterogeneity in definition of 'low to intermediate risk,' variable surgical experience across included studies, and inconsistent long-term follow-up limit generalizability. Randomized trial data remain sparse relative to the volume of observational data. Publication bias toward favorable outcomes cannot be excluded.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

pCND in cN0 PTC should be selectively offered to intermediate-risk patients (larger primaries, multifocal, with features of extrathyroidal extension) but routine pCND for low-risk microcarcinoma is not supported by the pooled evidence; shared decision-making should explicitly incorporate surgeon volume, institutional complication rates, and patient preference regarding reoperation risk.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis in British Journal of Surgery directly addressing one of thyroid surgery's most contested decisions—prophylactic CND in cN0 PTC—incorporating available randomized evidence; provides outcome-stratified data on recurrence, hypoparathyroidism, and RLN injury that are directly applicable to the informed consent and operative planning conversation.
