RCTPhase 3NEJM

Grunstein RR, Somers VK, Azarbarzin A, Malhotra A, Sands SA, Lucey BP, Allen SE, Giblin K, Falcon BL, Park SY, Lou J, Krueger K … TRIUMPH-1-OSA and TRIUMPH-2-OSA Investigators

BACKGROUND

Retatrutide is a once-weekly subcutaneous triple agonist of GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors under investigation for obesity and metabolic conditions. OSA affects >1 billion people and PAP adherence remains a persistent challenge. Whether pharmacologic weight reduction at the magnitude achievable with retatrutide would meaningfully reduce OSA severity was unknown.

METHODS

Two phase 3 double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trials (TRIUMPH-1-OSA, n=243, without T2DM; TRIUMPH-2-OSA, n=448, with T2DM) assigned adults with moderate-to-severe OSA (BMI ≥27) to once-weekly retatrutide 4 mg, 9 mg, or 12 mg vs placebo for 80 weeks. Primary endpoint was AHI change at 80 weeks for 9 mg and 12 mg groups using a hybrid-treatment estimand.

RESULTS

In TRIUMPH-1-OSA, retatrutide 9 mg reduced AHI by 34.3 events/hr vs 9.9 with placebo (Δ −24.4; 95% CI −34.1 to −14.7). In TRIUMPH-2-OSA, AHI reduction was 24.7 vs 10.3 events/hr (Δ −14.4; 95% CI −21.6 to −7.2). Body weight fell 12–22% with retatrutide vs 5–6% with placebo. Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse effects.

LIMITATIONS

80-week follow-up; outcomes beyond cessation unknown. Primarily obese patients — applicability to non-obese OSA unclear. AHI as endpoint may not capture all dimensions of OSA severity. Simultaneous weight loss makes it difficult to isolate a direct airway effect. Industry-funded.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Retatrutide demonstrates the largest pharmacologic AHI reduction published to date and may offer a disease-modifying option for obese OSA patients who fail or refuse PAP. For sleep surgeons, this raises the practical question of when to defer or sequence surgical therapy with emerging pharmacotherapy. Watch for FDA approval timeline and cost/access considerations.

Why score 5: Two large phase 3 double-blind RCTs published in NEJM demonstrate that retatrutide — a novel triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon receptor agonist — significantly reduces AHI vs placebo in moderate-to-severe OSA with obesity. The magnitude of AHI reduction (up to 34 events/hr) and simultaneous ~20% body-weight loss represent a potential paradigm shift in non-PAP OSA management.

Tham T, Lee JB, Megwalu UC … Megwalu UC (senior, Division of Comprehensive Otolaryngology, Dept of Otolaryngology-HNS, Stanford University, Palo Alto, California.)

BACKGROUND

Postoperative pain in otolaryngology — especially tonsillectomy, sleep surgery, and major head and neck procedures — drives substantial opioid prescribing. Suzetrigine is a first-in-class NaV1.8-selective sodium channel inhibitor recently approved by the FDA, offering peripheral analgesia without opioid-related respiratory depression, sedation, or misuse potential.

METHODS

Narrative review of pharmacology, phase 2 and phase 3 RCT evidence (>3000 patients across 5 trials using abdominoplasty and bunionectomy pain models), safety data, and one retrospective cohort of free flap and TORS patients. Synthesizes implications for otolaryngology practice.

RESULTS

Suzetrigine demonstrated superior analgesia vs placebo and mixed efficacy vs hydrocodone/acetaminophen across surgical pain models. Adverse event rates were similar to placebo, with no meaningful respiratory, cardiac, or sedating effects. One retrospective otolaryngology cohort showed feasibility in free flap and TORS settings.

LIMITATIONS

No RCT data yet in ENT-specific pain models (tonsillectomy, airway surgery). Evidence from abdominoplasty/bunionectomy may not generalize to mucosal or deep neck pain. Cost not established at time of writing; insurance formulary status unclear.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Suzetrigine is now FDA-approved and deserves consideration as an opioid-sparing component of multimodal analgesia for tonsillectomy, sleep surgery, and major H&N procedures — particularly in patients with opioid use disorder or opioid-related risk factors. Prospective ENT-specific RCTs are urgently warranted.

Why score 4: Review from a Stanford otolaryngology group synthesizes evidence on suzetrigine — the first FDA-approved NaV1.8-selective inhibitor — and its specific applicability to ENT procedures. Timely and practice-relevant given the opioid epidemic context; JAMA Otolaryngology is a flagship specialty journal.

Lin Y, Zheng Y, Ye W, Tan Z, Jia S, Sun M, Feng X, He H … He H (senior, Dept of Anesthesiology, General Hospital of Southern Theater Command of PLA, Guangzhou, China.)

BACKGROUND

Surgical field visibility during endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) is critical for safety and efficiency. Hyperventilation is used to reduce venous pressure and bleeding, but concerns exist about cerebral hypoperfusion. Dexmedetomidine, an α2-adrenergic agonist, provides additional sympatholysis without respiratory depression, but its safety on cerebral oxygenation during ESS was unconfirmed.

METHODS

Double-blind RCT of 74 patients with chronic rhinosinusitis undergoing ESS, randomized to dexmedetomidine + hyperventilation versus hyperventilation alone. Primary endpoint: Boezaart surgical field score at 30 minutes. Secondary endpoints: cerebral oxygen saturation (ScO₂) by near-infrared spectroscopy, mean arterial pressure, heart rate, and blood loss.

RESULTS

Dexmedetomidine + hyperventilation yielded a median Boezaart score of 2.0 vs 3.0 with hyperventilation alone (p = 0.003). Longitudinal analysis confirmed sustained improvement (OR 0.18; 95% CI 0.062–0.524; p = 0.002). ScO₂ was maintained without significant difference between groups. Bleeding rate was lower in the dexmedetomidine group.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study from a PLA hospital in China; external validity to other anesthetic protocols may be limited. Sample size relatively small (n=74). Long-term cognitive outcomes not measured.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Adding dexmedetomidine to a hypotensive ventilation protocol for ESS improves surgical field visibility without compromising cerebral oxygenation. Rhinologists and their anesthesia partners can consider this combination for complex FESS cases or surgeons with difficulty managing field bleeding.

Why score 4: Double-blind RCT directly addresses a common intraoperative challenge in FESS: balancing field visibility with cerebral safety. Favorable result with objective cerebral oximetry monitoring provides reassurance that dexmedetomidine augmentation of hypotensive technique is safe and effective.

Howser LA, Soler ZM, LaPointe KA, Durgham R, Heiland L, Salvador C, Schlosser RJ, Mattos JL … Mattos JL (senior, Dept of Otolaryngology-HNS, University of Virginia School of Medicine, Charlottesville, VA, USA.)

BACKGROUND

Olfactory dysfunction affects 60–80% of patients with CRSwNP. Dupilumab demonstrably improves olfaction in many patients, but response is variable and the biologic is expensive. Identifying which patients will benefit olfactorily before committing to treatment would improve both care and resource allocation.

METHODS

Single-arm prospective study of CRSwNP patients initiating dupilumab. Duration of olfactory loss, steroid responsiveness, and baseline smell fluctuation were recorded at baseline. Sniffin' Sticks olfactory testing (TDI score) and patient-reported smell were assessed at baseline and after 12 weeks. Regression analysis evaluated predictors of TDI improvement.

RESULTS

Shorter duration of olfactory loss correlated significantly with greater TDI improvement (R correlation statistically significant). A threshold of 10 years was identified: patients with >10 years of olfactory loss were less likely to achieve clinically meaningful improvement. Steroid responsiveness provided additional prognostic value beyond duration alone.

LIMITATIONS

Single-arm design without a control group; 12-week follow-up only; no blinding. Sample size and exact n not reported in the abstract. Single-center study.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Before initiating dupilumab for CRSwNP, document how long the patient has had anosmia and whether they responded to systemic steroids. Patients with >10 years of olfactory loss are less likely to regain meaningful smell from dupilumab — this should inform realistic counseling and shared decision-making.

Why score 4: Provides the first prospective evidence that readily available clinical variables — duration of olfactory loss and steroid responsiveness — can predict dupilumab olfactory response in CRSwNP. Actionable for counseling prior to initiating expensive biologic therapy.

ProspectiveDiagnostic AccuracyTelehealth

Livneh N, Wazana Y, Peled I, Carmel E, Peled C, Madgar O … Madgar O (senior, Dept of Otolaryngology-HNS, Sheba Medical Center, Ramat Gan, Israel.)

BACKGROUND

Sleep-disordered breathing (SDB) from tonsillar hypertrophy is common in children. Tonsil size grading on the Brodsky scale drives surgical planning, but in-person assessment is a barrier in telehealth workflows. TytoCare is a handheld FDA-cleared telemedicine device allowing parent-captured oropharyngeal imaging.

METHODS

Prospective diagnostic accuracy study at a tertiary pediatric ENT center. 169 children scheduled for tonsil/adenoid surgery had parents capture oropharyngeal videos using TytoCare. Three blinded pediatric otolaryngologists independently rated Brodsky scale tonsil size. Agreement, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV were calculated vs in-person exam as reference standard.

RESULTS

Interrater agreement was strong (Kendall's W = 0.88; dichotomized Fleiss κ = 0.65). Sensitivity was 70%, specificity 86%, PPV 84%, NPV 73% vs in-person exam. 12% of videos were unusable by all evaluators; 32% unusable by at least one. Failure rates were higher in younger children.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study from Israel; in-person exam used as reference standard (also imperfect). 12% complete video failure rate is clinically meaningful for workflows. Younger children had worse video quality, limiting utility in the age group most likely to need surgery.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Parent-captured videos via TytoCare are feasible for remote tonsil size grading with moderate-to-high accuracy, but a 12% complete failure rate and reduced reliability in young children mean telemedicine-based Brodsky grading cannot yet fully substitute for in-person assessment. Best used for triage of older, cooperative children; follow up in-person for surgical planning.

Why score 4: Prospective diagnostic accuracy study using a validated device in the flagship ENT journal. Telehealth assessment of tonsil size is a pressing practical question post-pandemic. Strong interrater agreement and favorable sensitivity/specificity support feasibility, though limitations exist in young children.
