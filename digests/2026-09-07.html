Impact of Intraoperative Patient-Specific 3D-Printed Temporal Bones: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Omari A, Nwosu OI, Andersen SAW, et al. … Andersen SAW (Massachusetts Eye and Ear, Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA)

BACKGROUND

3D-printed patient-specific temporal bone (TB) models have been proposed as intraoperative aids during cochlear implantation (CI) to reduce surgical cognitive load and potentially improve outcomes. Prior evidence was limited to observational studies. This RCT provides the first controlled evidence.

METHODS

40 consecutive adult primary CI surgeries at a large CI center were randomized 1:1: standard imaging alone versus standard imaging plus intraoperative inspection of a patient-specific 3D-printed TB model. Seven attending and five trainee surgeons participated. Primary outcome was intraoperative cognitive load (NASA-TLX tool).

RESULTS

Intraoperative cognitive load was significantly lower in the 3D-model arm across both attending and trainee surgeons. Secondary outcomes (operative time, electrode positioning, complications) did not differ significantly, though the trend favored the 3D-model group for trainees.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study with n=40 limits statistical power for clinical outcomes. 3D-model fabrication time and cost not formally analyzed. Generalizability to centers without rapid prototyping infrastructure is uncertain.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Patient-specific 3D-printed temporal bone models reduce intraoperative cognitive load during cochlear implantation. ENT training programs and high-volume CI centers should consider integrating this technology, particularly for complex anatomy cases and trainees.

Why score 4: RCT (n=40 CI surgeries) from MEEI/Harvard — the field's premier cochlear implant center — testing 3D-printed patient-specific temporal bone models as operative adjuncts. RCT design in Laryngoscope flagship journal from a top-tier institution earns a 4; n=40 limits generalizability slightly.

Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists for Obstructive Sleep Apnea: A Review

Harris A, Kaffenberger T … Kaffenberger T (Department of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery, University of Pittsburgh Medical Center, Pittsburgh, PA)

BACKGROUND

OSA affects nearly 1 billion adults worldwide and is independently associated with cardiovascular disease, stroke, and increased mortality. CPAP adherence is only 30–60%, and obesity is present in 60–70% of OSA patients. GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) have demonstrated substantial weight loss and are now FDA-approved for obesity — raising the question of whether they can meaningfully reduce OSA severity.

METHODS

Narrative review synthesizing available RCT and observational data on GLP-1 RAs (semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide) and OSA outcomes, including AHI reduction, hypoxic burden, CPAP pressure requirements, and body weight changes. The review also addresses implications for surgical candidacy and perioperative management.

RESULTS

GLP-1 RAs reduce AHI by 30–50% on average in obese OSA patients, with tirzepatide showing the largest effect (SURMOUNT-OSA trial: ~65% AHI reduction from baseline). Some patients achieve AHI < 5 events/hr and may no longer meet PAP therapy indications. Weight loss of 15–20% appears necessary for meaningful OSA improvement.

LIMITATIONS

Long-term OSA outcomes beyond 12 months are not yet established. Weight regain after GLP-1 RA discontinuation may restore OSA. Not all OSA is obesity-driven; non-obese patients with craniofacial or neuromuscular etiologies will not benefit. Drug cost and insurance access remain barriers.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

GLP-1 receptor agonists should now be part of the ENT/sleep surgeon's conversation with obese OSA patients. Consider a 3–6 month pharmacotherapy trial before surgical airway intervention in eligible patients. Patients who achieve sufficient weight loss and AHI normalization may defer or avoid surgery. Coordinate with obesity medicine or endocrinology.

Why score 4: Flagship specialty journal (JAMA Oto) review of GLP-1 receptor agonists for OSA — directly relevant to surgical decision-making and referral patterns. With semaglutide/tirzepatide data rapidly accumulating, this review provides a practical framework for ENT surgeons managing OSA patients seeking or delaying surgery.

Compartment-Specific Recurrence in Medullary Thyroid Cancer

Seo YJ, Larios KN, Mao YV, et al. … Mao YV (Section of Endocrine Surgery, UCLA David Geffen School of Medicine, Los Angeles, CA)

BACKGROUND

Total thyroidectomy with bilateral central neck dissection (CND) is the guideline-recommended operation for medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), yet the benefit of contralateral prophylactic CND (pCND) remains debated — particularly when ipsilateral nodal disease is confined. Practice varies substantially across centers.

METHODS

Retrospective analysis of patients with MTC undergoing total thyroidectomy with nodal dissection at UCLA. Compartment-specific recurrence (central vs. lateral, ipsilateral vs. contralateral) was the primary outcome, stratified by initial extent of dissection and pathologic nodal status.

RESULTS

Contralateral central compartment recurrence was rare in patients with pathologically negative ipsilateral central nodes, supporting selective rather than routine bilateral pCND in node-negative disease. Patients with ipsilateral N1a disease had higher rates of contralateral nodal recurrence, supporting bilateral dissection in this group.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective design with referral bias at a tertiary center. Biochemical recurrence (calcitonin/CEA) not fully correlated with compartment-specific recurrence in all cases. Follow-up duration varied.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

A compartment-specific, risk-stratified approach to CND in MTC is supported by these data: bilateral pCND appears justified when ipsilateral nodal disease is confirmed, but may be omitted in truly node-negative central compartments. Discuss prospectively at multidisciplinary tumor boards.

Why score 4: Addresses a live surgical debate (unilateral vs. bilateral CND in MTC) in JAMA Oto from UCLA's endocrine surgery program. Compartment-level recurrence analysis fills an evidence gap in MTC management guidelines and will directly influence operative planning.

Tissue-Informed ctDNA Assay Performance in Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Sicard RM, Cooke PV, Steinbaum A, et al. … Steinbaum A (Department of Otolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery, Mount Sinai Hospital, New York, NY)

BACKGROUND

Liquid biopsy with circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) has transformed surveillance in HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancers. In HPV-negative HNSCC, no broadly shared mutation target exists, necessitating patient-specific, tissue-informed ctDNA assays. Evidence for these assays in HPV-negative disease is sparse.

METHODS

Prospective cohort study at Mount Sinai of patients with HPV-negative HNSCC undergoing tissue-informed ctDNA testing at diagnosis and during post-treatment surveillance. Diagnostic sensitivity, specificity, and lead time over clinical/radiographic recurrence were analyzed.

RESULTS

Tissue-informed ctDNA detected HPV-negative HNSCC recurrence with high specificity and substantial lead time over imaging (median 3–4 months). Pre-treatment ctDNA levels correlated with tumor burden. Clearance after definitive treatment was associated with favorable outcomes. False-positive rates were low with matched tissue profiling.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study; small n in some subgroup analyses. Cost and turnaround time of tissue-informed assays (vs. off-the-shelf panels) remain barriers. Assay availability limited to academic centers with genomics infrastructure.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Tissue-informed ctDNA assays should be integrated into post-treatment surveillance protocols for HPV-negative HNSCC at institutions with access. Detectable ctDNA at end-of-treatment or during follow-up should trigger early salvage workup. This is the next surveillance frontier for non-HPV head and neck cancer.

Why score 4: Prospective liquid biopsy performance data in HPV-negative HNSCC — a critical gap because tissue-agnostic ctDNA assays are inadequate here. JAMA Oto flagship journal, Mount Sinai HN oncology program. This will shape how surgeons monitor HPV-negative patients post-treatment.

Treatment of Congenital Cytomegalovirus Beyond the Neonatal Period: A Single-Arm Meta-Analysis

Cabral TDD, Mafra M, Plane J, et al. … Plane J (Department of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery, University of Minnesota Medical School, Minneapolis, MN)

BACKGROUND

Congenital cytomegalovirus (cCMV) is the leading non-genetic cause of sensorineural hearing loss in children. Antiviral treatment with valganciclovir within the neonatal period (first month) is established, but most cCMV is diagnosed later. Whether treatment beyond the neonatal period improves hearing outcomes is unclear.

METHODS

Systematic review and single-arm meta-analysis of studies reporting antiviral treatment (ganciclovir or valganciclovir) for cCMV initiated after the first month of life. Databases searched through mid-2026. Primary outcome: audiologic improvement or stabilization at last follow-up.

RESULTS

Postneonatal antiviral treatment was associated with hearing stabilization or improvement in a majority of treated ears, though effect sizes varied by age at treatment initiation and hearing loss severity. Earlier treatment (first 6 months) showed more favorable results than treatment initiated after 1 year.

LIMITATIONS

All included studies were case series (Level IV evidence); no RCT data exist for postneonatal treatment. Heterogeneity in treatment duration and hearing outcome measurement methods limits pooled estimates. Publication bias cannot be excluded.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Antiviral therapy (valganciclovir) appears to stabilize or improve hearing in cCMV-diagnosed infants beyond the neonatal period, particularly when started within the first 6 months of life. ENT and audiology clinics should screen for cCMV at hearing loss diagnosis and consider antiviral treatment in newly diagnosed infants regardless of age, pending full newborn CMV screening rollout.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis (highest evidence tier) addressing a directly actionable question: should antiviral therapy be offered to cCMV-diagnosed infants beyond the neonatal window? Laryngoscope flagship journal, University of Minnesota. Fills a real evidence gap for pediatric audiology/ENT practice.
