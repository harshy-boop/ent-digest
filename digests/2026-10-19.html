Executive Summary of the Clinical Practice Guideline on Tinnitus Update.

Adams ME, Cramer JD, Tunkel DE, et al. … Akrami Z (senior, American Academy of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery Foundation, Alexandria,)

BACKGROUND

Tinnitus affects an estimated 10-15% of U.S. adults; persistent, bothersome tinnitus significantly impairs quality of life. This is an update to the prior AAO-HNS Foundation tinnitus guideline, reflecting new evidence on evaluation, education, and management.

METHODS

Evidence-based guideline development by multidisciplinary panel using systematic literature review; statements graded by strength of evidence. The focus is on patients ≥18 years with persistent (≥6 months), bothersome tinnitus.

RESULTS

Updated recommendations cover history and physical examination, audiologic evaluation, tinnitus questionnaires, CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), sound therapy, hearing amplification, and counseling on conditions to avoid (e.g., routine imaging for bilateral non-pulsatile tinnitus). New guidance added on shared decision-making and treatment individualization.

LIMITATIONS

Evidence base for many tinnitus treatments remains limited by heterogeneous study designs; guideline recommends against routine use of several popular but unvalidated therapies.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Clinicians should offer validated questionnaires (e.g., THI, TFI) at initial evaluation, refer for audiologic testing in all cases of persistent tinnitus, provide counseling/education, and offer CBT as the highest-evidence behavioral intervention. Avoid routine MRI/CT for symmetric, non-pulsatile tinnitus without red flags.

Why score 5: Updated AAO-HNS Clinical Practice Guideline for tinnitus published in the flagship ENT specialty journal. Affects management of persistent, bothersome tinnitus in >50M US adults. Sets new evidence-based recommendations for evaluation and treatment, directly changing Monday-morning practice across all clinician types.

Cochlear Implantation and Cognitive Function in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.

Joyce CA, Zhang K, Stultiens JJA, et al. … Quimby AE (senior, Department of Surgery, Division of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Dalhous)

BACKGROUND

Hearing loss is an established risk factor for cognitive decline and dementia. Cochlear implantation restores auditory input; whether this translates into measurable cognitive improvement is not firmly established. This SR/MA examines objective cognitive testing before and after CI.

METHODS

Comprehensive literature search across MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane, Web of Science, PsycINFO, and CINAHL. Included English-language studies with objective pre- and post-CI cognitive testing (minimum 6 months follow-up) in adults with postlingual hearing loss. Interventional studies, cohorts, case-controls, and case series included.

RESULTS

Data from multiple studies show statistically significant improvements in cognitive scores following CI, particularly in domains of attention/processing speed and global cognition. Effect sizes were moderate. Improvements were most consistent at the 12-month mark.

LIMITATIONS

High heterogeneity in cognitive test batteries used across studies limits direct comparison. Most studies lacked untreated hearing-loss control groups. Publication bias possible.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Adults considering cochlear implantation can now be counseled that CI is associated with measurable improvements in cognitive function within the first year, adding a neurological benefit argument alongside audiological outcomes.

Why score 4: Systematic review and meta-analysis in Otology & Neurotology—the premier otology journal—addressing whether CI improves cognitive outcomes in adults. Addresses an increasingly asked counseling question as the hearing-dementia link grows in prominence. Solid SR methodology across multiple cognitive domains.

Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis

Systemic outcomes following maxillomandibular advancement for obstructive sleep apnea: systematic review and meta-analysis.

Holmes C, Namavarian A, Malinsky R, et al. … Ibrahim B (senior, Department of Otolaryngology - Head and Neck Surgery, Université de Montréal, Mo)

BACKGROUND

Obstructive sleep apnea is associated with elevated risk of cardiovascular disease, hypertension, insulin resistance, and systemic inflammation. MMA is an effective surgical cure for OSA, but whether it improves systemic comorbidities—not just AHI—is poorly characterized.

METHODS

Comprehensive literature search (PubMed and additional databases) for studies evaluating systemic outcomes after MMA in OSA patients. Outcomes included blood pressure, cardiometabolic markers, inflammatory biomarkers, glycemic indices, and quality of life.

RESULTS

MMA was associated with significant improvements in blood pressure, daytime sleepiness (ESS), and quality of life. Trends toward improvement in cardiometabolic markers and C-reactive protein. Extent of systemic benefit correlated with degree of AHI improvement.

LIMITATIONS

Included studies are predominantly retrospective with short follow-up. Heterogeneity in outcome measures and patient populations. Absence of randomized controlled data.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Surgeons and sleep physicians can now cite SR-level evidence that MMA improves not just AHI but blood pressure and quality of life, strengthening the case for surgical intervention in patients who fail or decline CPAP.

Why score 4: First comprehensive SR/MA synthesizing systemic health outcomes (cardiovascular, metabolic, glycemic, inflammatory) of maxillomandibular advancement for OSA. Published in IJOMS. Directly strengthens the sleep surgeon's argument for MMA as a disease-modifying intervention beyond AHI reduction.

Individual Patient Data Meta-Analysis

Efficacy of Systemic Bevacizumab in Juvenile-Onset Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis: An Individual Patient Data Meta-Analysis.

Hortobagyi D, Denk-Linnert DM, Altmann M, et al. … Kranebitter V (senior, Division of Phoniatrics and Speech Language Therapy, Department of Otorhinolaryn)

BACKGROUND

Juvenile-onset recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (joRRP) is an HPV-driven disease of the pediatric airway requiring repeated surgical debulking. Persistent dysphonia, airway compromise, and psychological morbidity are hallmarks. Systemic bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) has emerged as an adjuvant, but individual-level evidence was lacking.

METHODS

Individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis pooling patient-level data from multiple cohorts of joRRP cases treated with systemic bevacizumab. IPD methodology allows for patient-level covariate adjustment, subgroup analyses by disease severity, and pooled time-to-event analyses not possible with aggregate-data meta-analysis.

RESULTS

Systemic bevacizumab significantly extended the inter-surgical interval and reduced annual papilloma scores in joRRP patients. Response was most pronounced in severe, multi-site disease. Toxicity (hypertension, proteinuria) was manageable at doses used.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective patient populations with heterogeneous HPV types and prior treatment burdens. No RCT data; selection bias toward severe cases. Long-term safety in growing children requires further study.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

IPD-level evidence supports systemic bevacizumab as an adjuvant therapy for joRRP with severe/recurrent disease, significantly reducing surgical burden. Consider in patients requiring >4 procedures/year or with multi-site/extensive papilloma load.

Why score 4: Individual patient data meta-analysis—the highest level of evidence synthesis—for systemic bevacizumab in juvenile-onset RRP, a rare and surgically demanding pediatric airway condition with very limited adjuvant options. Published in Journal of Voice (leading laryngology journal). Directly guides adjuvant therapy decisions in complex RRP cases.

Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis

Management of Acquired Velopharyngeal Insufficiency in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Kothari DS, Abiri A, Clair JM, et al. … Vukkadala N (senior, Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Cedars-Sinai Medical Center,)

BACKGROUND

Acquired velopharyngeal insufficiency (VPI) in adults can result from post-oncologic defects, neurologic injury, or prior nasopharyngeal surgery. Unlike cleft-associated VPI, the adult non-cleft form is poorly studied with no established treatment algorithm.

METHODS

PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library searched for studies from January 2005 to October 2025 on adults (≥18 years) with non-cleft acquired VPI, with speech and/or swallowing outcomes. Two independent reviewers; random-effects meta-analysis.

RESULTS

Pooled rates of improved speech intelligibility and reduced hypernasality were demonstrated across surgical interventions (pharyngeal flaps, sphincter pharyngoplasty, augmentation/injection). Outcomes varied significantly by etiology. Swallowing outcomes were less well-reported.

LIMITATIONS

High heterogeneity in intervention types, outcome measures, and follow-up periods. Most studies retrospective with small samples. No comparative RCT data available.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This SR provides the most comprehensive treatment outcome synthesis available for adult acquired VPI and supports surgical repair in appropriate candidates, with pharyngeal flaps and sphincter pharyngoplasty showing highest success rates for speech. Swallowing outcomes remain understudied.

Why score 4: SR/meta-analysis in The Laryngoscope (flagship ENT journal) from Cedars-Sinai, synthesizing treatment outcomes for acquired (non-cleft) VPI in adults—a challenging condition without consensus management. Pooled speech and swallowing outcome data across surgical and non-surgical interventions.
