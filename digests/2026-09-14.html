Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo.

Purks JL, Dubal DB, Okun MS … Okun MS (senior, Department of Neurology, Norman Fixel Institute for Neurological Diseases, University of Florida, Gainesville.)

BACKGROUND

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is the most common cause of vertigo in the general population, affecting ~2.4% of people over a lifetime. Despite highly effective repositioning maneuvers (Epley, Semont), BPPV remains underdiagnosed and undertreated across primary care and specialist settings.

METHODS

JAMA article by Purks JL, Dubal DB, and Okun MS (Norman Fixel Institute for Neurological Diseases, University of Florida). Authorship reflects a neurological-ENT crossover perspective on a condition predominantly managed by otolaryngologists — making cross-disciplinary consensus visible.

RESULTS

Full abstract was not indexed at time of digest generation. Given the journal, authors, and JAMA format, this is most likely a JAMA Review or Rational Clinical Examination–style article synthesizing BPPV diagnosis and management. Retrieve full text from JAMA directly via DOI or PubMed link.

LIMITATIONS

Abstract not yet indexed. Cross-disciplinary authorship (neurology-led) may de-emphasize nuances of posterior-canal vs. lateral-canal BPPV repositioning that are central to ENT practice. Clinical conclusions should be drawn from the full publication.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Retrieve the full text. JAMA articles on BPPV shape how referring PCPs and neurologists counsel patients — knowing the published framework lets you align conversations and correct misconceptions efficiently. ENT-specific nuances (canal variant identification, Dix-Hallpike technique, recurrence counseling) remain yours to reinforce.

Efficacy and Predictors of Response to Biologic Therapy and Endoscopic Sinus Surgery in Patients With Nasal Polyps: A Systematic Review.

Chakravorty A, Lin ME, Lubner R, Mace JC et al. … Beswick DM (senior, Department of Head and Neck Surgery, David Geffen School of Medicine at UCLA, Los Angeles, California, USA.)

BACKGROUND

Refractory CRSwNP is now treated with both endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) and IL-4/13, IL-5, IgE, or TSLP–targeting biologics. Head-to-head comparative trials remain scarce, leaving rhinologists without clear patient-selection criteria. This UCLA-led synthesis fills that gap.

METHODS

David Geffen School of Medicine at UCLA (Beswick DM, senior). Systematic review and indirect comparison of 10 Phase-3 biologic RCTs, 2 pragmatic ESS trials, and 1 head-to-head biologic trial (n=3775 total). Primary outcomes: SNOT-22 and nasal polyp score (NPS) effect sizes and subgroup interactions. PRISMA-compliant, searched through December 2025.

RESULTS

By indirect comparison, tezepelumab produced the largest SNOT-22 improvement (−27.4) and dupilumab the next largest (−17 to −21). Mepolizumab and omalizumab showed smaller effects. ESS effect sizes were comparable to mid-tier biologics. Subgroup predictors of differential response (eosinophil counts, prior surgery history) were synthesized.

LIMITATIONS

Indirect comparison — no head-to-head biologic vs. ESS RCT yet exists. Trial populations differ in disease severity and prior surgery rates. SNOT-22 is patient-reported; inter-trial variation in baseline scores affects cross-study effect-size comparisons. Long-term durability data for biologics remain limited.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

When counseling refractory CRSwNP patients, tezepelumab and dupilumab offer the strongest symptom and polyp-burden reduction among current biologics. ESS remains competitive for anatomic disease or biologic non-response. Use this synthesis to anchor shared decision-making and to support prior-authorization arguments for higher-efficacy agents.

Why score 4: Flagship rhinology journal (IFAR), largest synthesis to date of 13 RCTs (n=3775) comparing biologic efficacy and ESS in CRSwNP. Tezepelumab and dupilumab emerge as highest-effect biologics (SNOT-22 −27 and −17 to −21 respectively), with subgroup predictors synthesized. Directly informs the most-debated management question in rhinology today — biologic vs. surgery — and will shape shared decision-making conversations this week.

Vestibular Rehabilitation for Vestibular Migraine: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Booth L, Mageto R, Assadzadeh GE, Lui J … Lui J (senior, Department of Surgery, Section of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Cumming School of Medicine, University of Calgary, Calgary, Alberta, Canada.)

BACKGROUND

Vestibular migraine (VM) is one of the most common causes of recurrent vertigo in adults, yet the evidence base for vestibular rehabilitation (VR) has been limited to small, heterogeneous trials. Clinicians frequently face uncertainty about whether to refer VM patients to vestibular PT alongside migraine prophylaxis.

METHODS

University of Calgary / Cumming School of Medicine (Lui J, senior). Systematic review of Medline, Embase, and Scopus from inception through May 2025. Included adults with VM diagnosed by ICHD-3 criteria. Seven studies, n=272. Mixed-effects meta-analysis of DHI and ABC. PRISMA-2020 compliant.

RESULTS

All 7 studies demonstrated DHI improvement after VR. Individual study treatment effects ranged from −10.6 to −44.7 points. Meta-analysis showed a clinically meaningful aggregate DHI reduction (exceeding the MCID of 18 points in the pooled estimate). ABC scores also improved. No serious adverse effects of VR were reported across any study.

LIMITATIONS

n=272 across 7 studies is modest. Significant heterogeneity in VR protocols (exercise intensity, duration, home vs. clinic). No sham-controlled design in any included study; placebo effect cannot be excluded. VM diagnostic criteria varied slightly across older studies.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Offer vestibular rehabilitation to adults with vestibular migraine — pooled evidence now supports clinically meaningful dizziness handicap reduction. Refer to vestibular-certified PTs. This strengthens the case for non-pharmacologic co-management alongside migraine prophylaxis and supports authorization requests for vestibular PT in this population.

Why score 4: Flagship journal (Otology & Neurotology), rigorous PRISMA-2020 systematic review and mixed-effects meta-analysis across 7 studies (n=272). Demonstrates clinically meaningful DHI reduction with vestibular rehabilitation in vestibular migraine — a diagnosis squarely in neurotology practice for which non-pharmacologic evidence has lagged. Will influence referral patterns to vestibular-certified PTs today.
