Efficacy and Predictors of Response to Biologic Therapy and Endoscopic Sinus Surgery in Patients With Nasal Polyps: A Systematic Review

Chakravorty A, Lin ME, Lubner R, Mace JC … Beswick DM (senior, Dept. of Head and Neck Surgery, David Geffen School of Medicine at UCLA, Los Angeles, CA)

BACKGROUND

Biologics (dupilumab, tezepelumab, omalizumab, mepolizumab, benralizumab, depemokimab) and endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) are both evidence-based for refractory chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyposis (CRSwNP), but no head-to-head trial compares them. Clinicians face daily decisions about sequencing or combining these modalities without a unified evidence base.

METHODS

PubMed systematic review through December 2025 of RCTs for CRSwNP. Thirteen trials (10 biologic phase-3 placebo-controlled RCTs, 2 pragmatic ESS trials, 1 head-to-head biologic trial; n=3775) were included. SNOT-22 and nasal polyp score (NPS) effect sizes and subgroup predictors were extracted and compared by indirect methods.

RESULTS

Tezepelumab produced the largest effect (SNOT-22 −27.4, NPS −2.1); dupilumab was close (SNOT-22 −17 to −21, NPS −1.7 to −2.1). Mepolizumab/omalizumab showed intermediate effects (SNOT-22 −10.6 to −16.5). Benralizumab improved NPS but not SNOT-22; depemokimab's effect was below the minimal clinically important difference. ESS effects varied widely by trial and follow-up (SNOT-22 −21.9 at 6 months in MACRO vs. −4.9 at 12 months in PolypESS). Predictors differed by agent: eosinophilia, NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease, and prior ESS favored dupilumab; asthma favored benralizumab; baseline severity favored ESS; tezepelumab and omalizumab benefited patients regardless of subgroup.

LIMITATIONS

Indirect comparison only—no head-to-head biologic vs. ESS trial exists. Heterogeneous baseline severity, follow-up duration, and comparator arms limit cross-trial inference. Long-term durability and combination therapy data remain scarce.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

When choosing a biologic for CRSwNP, dupilumab and tezepelumab deliver the strongest and most consistent symptom relief; tezepelumab benefits patients regardless of eosinophilic phenotype, making it particularly useful for non-eosinophilic or aspirin-tolerant CRSwNP. ESS remains appropriate and effective especially for severity-driven or access-limited patients. Combination strategies and direct comparisons are still needed.

Why score 4: Largest synthesis to date of biologic vs ESS RCTs for CRSwNP (13 trials, n=3775) from a core rhinology journal with a top-tier academic senior author. Provides the most actionable comparative effect-size data yet for the central treatment-selection question in rhinology.

Prediction of Rhinologic Surgery Within 90 Days Using Item-Level SNOT-22 Responses: A Multisite Machine Learning Study of 35,170 Patients

Sramek M, Kumar N, Patel S, Miglani A … Lal D (senior, Dept. of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Mayo Clinic Arizona, Phoenix, AZ)

BACKGROUND

Rhinology clinics face growing demand with limited capacity. CT imaging is often required before surgical decision-making, creating bottlenecks. Patient-reported outcome measures like the SNOT-22 are already collected at triage but their predictive value for surgical selection has not been systematically exploited at scale.

METHODS

Retrospective ML study of 35,170 patients seen at 5 Mayo Clinic sites (2018–2025). Outcome: any rhinologic surgery within 90 days of initial visit. Item-level SNOT-22 responses plus demographics were used to train XGBoost, logistic regression, random forest, and TabNet models with 5-fold cross-validation. Hyperparameter optimization via Optuna. Held-out 20% validation cohort.

RESULTS

XGBoost achieved AUC 0.70 (0.69–0.71), outperforming all other models (p<0.001). At optimal threshold: sensitivity 66%, specificity 64%, PPV 28%, NPV 90%. Top predictors were age, nasal blockage, facial pain/pressure, and decreased smell/taste. Site-level surgical rates ranged 11.5%–27.4% with stable cross-site AUC.

LIMITATIONS

Single integrated health system limits generalizability to community or international settings. Moderate AUC (0.70) and low PPV (28%) mean the model cannot reliably predict who WILL need surgery, only who likely WON'T. Deployment would require prospective validation and human oversight integration.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

A 90% NPV for rhinologic surgery means this ML model—using only SNOT-22 items and demographics collected at intake—can safely flag patients who are unlikely to require an operation within 90 days, potentially deferring CT imaging and subspecialty visits for this group. Best used as an administrative triage adjunct in high-volume rhinology practices to reduce CT burden and optimize clinic efficiency, not as a gatekeeper for individual care decisions.

Why score 4: Largest ever ML triage study in rhinology, spanning 5 sites of an integrated academic health system. The 90% NPV enables confident deferral of patients unlikely to need surgery, addressing real-world capacity strain. Mayo Clinic senior author adds methodological and deployment credibility.

Accuracy of type 2 inflammatory biomarkers to diagnose asthma in adults and children: a meta-analysis of 30 studies with prospective validation in an adult primary care cohort

Roy MA, Gronnier M, Désy L, Pouliot L … Couillard S (senior, Dept. of Medicine, Université de Sherbrooke, QC, Canada; Leiden University Medical Center, Leiden, Netherlands)

BACKGROUND

Asthma is under-diagnosed in adults and children, partly because bronchial provocation testing (BPT) is expensive and inaccessible in primary care. Fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) and blood eosinophil count (BEC) have been proposed as office-based rule-in tests, but their exact diagnostic thresholds have lacked rigorous multisite validation.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 30 studies (22 adult/9 pediatric, 8130 total patients) comparing FeNO or BEC against BPT in patients with suspected asthma and normal spirometry. Primary outcome: AUC for diagnosing airway hyper-responsiveness. Pre-specified rule-in thresholds and post-hoc rule-out thresholds were derived. Prospective single-centre validation cohort (DIVE; n=123 BPT-referred adults from primary care) was used to replicate meta-analysis findings.

RESULTS

FeNO AUC was good in adults (0.80 [0.73–0.85]) and fair in children (0.75 [0.65–0.82]). In adults: FeNO ≥65 ppb achieved PPV 80% (rule-in); FeNO ≥48 ppb = LR+ 10.1; FeNO <20 ppb = NPV 93% (rule-out) with 18% missed diagnoses. BEC showed weak standalone accuracy (AUC 0.65) and added no value to FeNO. DIVE cohort validated these thresholds (AUC 0.77). FeNO accuracy fell within 2 weeks of ICS exposure.

LIMITATIONS

Pediatric validation cohort ongoing (adult DIVE only so far). Most included studies were clinic-based, not truly primary care. ICS exposure substantially degrades FeNO accuracy. BEC performance was poor—limiting utility of this widely available test.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For ENT/rhinology clinicians co-managing type 2 airway disease: FeNO ≥65 ppb is a reliable rule-in for asthma (PPV 80%) and FeNO <20 ppb safely rules it out (NPV 93%) in adults not recently on ICS. Blood eosinophils alone are an unreliable asthma diagnostic. In patients with CRSwNP referred for biologic therapy, FeNO-based asthma diagnosis can be incorporated without ordering a formal BPT, streamlining the combined upper/lower airway workup.

Why score 4: Lancet Respiratory Medicine meta-analysis with prospective validation provides the strongest evidence yet for FeNO-guided asthma diagnosis. ENT surgeons managing CRSwNP routinely encounter patients with concurrent type 2 airway disease; these validated thresholds are directly actionable in the combined upper/lower airway clinic workflow.

Twelve-Month Effectiveness of Dupilumab on Eosinophilic Otitis Media in Patients with Severe Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps: A Multicenter Observational Study

Galletti C, Stilo G, Sireci F, Giammona Indaco F … Galletti B (senior, Unit of Otorhinolaryngology, University of Messina, Italy)

BACKGROUND

Eosinophilic otitis media (EOM) is a refractory type 2 inflammatory disease of the middle ear frequently co-occurring with severe CRSwNP and asthma. Conventional otologic treatment (myringotomy, tubes, topical steroids) offers incomplete or transient relief. Since EOM and CRSwNP share IL-4/IL-13 pathway activation via the nasal–Eustachian tube–middle-ear continuum, dupilumab may offer systemic control of both compartments simultaneously.

METHODS

Multicenter Italian observational cohort of 48 adults with severe CRSwNP plus EOM (Iino diagnostic criteria). All received dupilumab 300 mg every 2 weeks. Primary endpoint: Chronic Otitis Media Outcome Test-15 (COMOT-15; 0–75). Secondary: tympanometry, Nasal Polyp Score, SNOT-22, nasal congestion VAS. Assessed at baseline, 6, and 12 months.

RESULTS

Median COMOT-15 decreased from 49 at baseline to 20 at 6 months and 15 at 12 months, representing a 70% reduction in otologic disease burden. Tympanometric curves and sinonasal outcomes (NPS, SNOT-22) showed parallel progressive improvement. No new safety signals were observed beyond those known for dupilumab.

LIMITATIONS

No control group (observational design), small n=48, single ethnicity Italian multicenter sample. EOM is rare and heterogeneous; patient selection was criterion-based but real-world. Effect on hearing thresholds and sensorineural function was not the primary focus.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

In patients with concomitant severe CRSwNP and EOM, dupilumab produces substantial, progressive 12-month improvement in middle-ear disease burden measurable by COMOT-15 and tympanometry—beyond its known sinonasal effects. Clinicians managing this phenotype (typically atopic, asthmatic, with bilateral middle-ear effusion/granulation) can consider dupilumab as the preferred systemic option when conventional otologic treatments have failed, with reasonable expectation of durable benefit.

Why score 4: Provides the largest and longest multicenter real-world data on dupilumab for eosinophilic otitis media—a challenging type 2 middle-ear condition lacking alternative systemic options. The COMOT-15 reduction from 49 to 15 at 12 months is clinically dramatic. This fills a critical evidence gap for an off-label indication with growing clinical demand.

Cost-effectiveness of Near-infrared Autofluorescence (NIRAF) in Total Thyroidectomy

Kulkarni P, Hollenbeak CS, Goldenberg D, Goyal N … Goyal N (senior, Dept. of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Penn State College of Medicine, Hershey, PA)

BACKGROUND

Hypocalcemia is the most common complication after total thyroidectomy, occurring in up to 30% transiently and 1–3% permanently, with significant morbidity and resource costs. Near-infrared autofluorescence (NIRAF) uses the intrinsic autofluorescence of parathyroid glands to aid intraoperative identification, potentially reducing inadvertent parathyroid sacrifice. Cost data have limited widespread adoption despite favorable clinical signals.

METHODS

Decision-tree cost-effectiveness model from a US third-party payor perspective over a 1-year time horizon. Probabilities of transient and permanent hypocalcemia, hospitalization costs, and QALYs for each health state were drawn from the published literature. Incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) was calculated vs. naked-eye visualization, with willingness-to-pay (WTP) threshold of $100,000/QALY. Deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses were performed.

RESULTS

Base case: Naked-eye thyroidectomy cost $122.51/patient vs. NIRAF at $566.99/patient over 1 year. NIRAF yielded more QALYs (0.994 vs. 0.989). ICER = $85,042/QALY—below the $100k WTP threshold. Sensitivity analyses maintained cost-effectiveness as long as NIRAF device/consumable cost remains below $578 per procedure. The model was most sensitive to the rate of permanent hypocalcemia and device price.

LIMITATIONS

Model-based analysis with uncertainty around true permanent hypocalcemia rate; short 1-year horizon may underestimate long-term permanent hypocalcemia burden. Single-country payor perspective (US); does not account for learning curve or differences in surgical volume or baseline complication rates.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

NIRAF is cost-effective for total thyroidectomy under US pricing—provided consumable/device costs stay below ~$578/case. Surgeons advocating for NIRAF adoption now have peer-reviewed economic data to support institutional purchase proposals and payor coverage discussions. This is particularly actionable at high-volume thyroid centers where device amortization lowers per-case cost. Watch for device pricing exceeding the threshold, which would flip cost-effectiveness.

Why score 4: First US cost-effectiveness analysis of NIRAF during total thyroidectomy, published in Annals of Surgery. ICER of $85,042/QALY—below the standard $100k willingness-to-pay threshold—provides the economic justification surgeons and institutions need to defend acquisition and reimbursement of this parathyroid-sparing technology.

Treatment-related lymphopenia predicts long-term survival in locally advanced head and neck cancer

This secondary analysis of a phase III RCT dataset (n=222) with a median 132-month follow-up evaluated treatment-related decline in absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) during preoperative radiation (±chemotherapy) in LA-HNSCC. Grade 3–4 lymphopenia occurred in 55% of patients; ALC decline >0.74×10⁹/L was an independent adverse prognostic factor for overall survival. A nomogram incorporating ALC decline performed favorably for individualized survival prediction. These findings support monitoring peripheral lymphocyte counts during induction chemoradiation for HNSCC as a low-cost, easily obtained adverse prognostic biomarker with potential value for treatment intensification decisions.
