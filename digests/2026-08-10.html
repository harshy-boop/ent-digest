The impact of corticosteroid therapy on clinical outcomes in paediatric deep neck space infections: A meta-analysis.

Ho E, Fuzi J, Popovic G et al. … Soma M (senior, Sydney Children's Hospital)

BACKGROUND

Pediatric deep neck space infections (DNSIs)—retropharyngeal and parapharyngeal abscesses—carry risks of airway obstruction, mediastinitis, and sepsis. Adjunct corticosteroid use alongside antibiotics is practiced variably, with unclear evidence for reducing surgical need.

METHODS

PRISMA-guided systematic review of 4 databases (January 1996–August 2024). Three studies comprising 2523 pediatric patients met inclusion criteria: 695 received antibiotics plus corticosteroids; 1828 received antibiotics alone. Primary outcome: surgical intervention rate. Random-effects meta-analysis.

RESULTS

Children who did NOT receive corticosteroids were significantly more likely to require surgical drainage (RR 2.07, 95% CI 1.43–2.99). Moderate heterogeneity was noted (I² not specified). No major adverse events attributed to corticosteroids were reported across included studies.

LIMITATIONS

Only 3 retrospective studies included; moderate heterogeneity; cannot exclude confounding by indication (more severely ill children may have been routed directly to surgery regardless of steroid use). No randomized evidence exists.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

In children with deep neck space infections managed medically with antibiotics, adjunct corticosteroids may roughly halve the likelihood of requiring surgical drainage. Clinicians should consider corticosteroid addition for appropriate DNSI patients; prospective randomized data are still needed to confirm benefit and define patient selection.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis of 3 studies (n=2523) showing a >2-fold higher surgical intervention rate in children with deep neck space infections not receiving corticosteroids (RR 2.07, 95% CI 1.43–2.99). Large pooled N and consistent directionality support adjunct corticosteroid use, pending prospective confirmation.

Glossopharyngeal nerve block versus tonsillar fossa ropivacaine infiltration within multimodal analgesia for pediatric post-tonsillectomy pain: a randomized controlled clinical trial.

Faramarzi M, Nemati M, Farbood A et al. … Faramarzi A (senior, Otolaryngology Research Center)

BACKGROUND

Post-tonsillectomy pain is a major challenge in pediatric day-surgery, contributing to delayed recovery and poor oral intake. Peritonsillar infiltration (RI) is widely used, but glossopharyngeal nerve block (GNB) offers a broader anatomic coverage of the tonsillar fossa innervation.

METHODS

Double-blind randomized controlled trial. 90 children aged 4–10 undergoing day-case tonsillectomy at Shiraz University of Medical Sciences were randomized 1:1:1 to bilateral GNB with 0.2% ropivacaine, peritonsillar RI with 0.2% ropivacaine, or sham control. Primary outcome: PACU FLACC pain score. Secondary outcomes: pain at rest and swallowing (2–24h), time to first rescue analgesia, nausea, sleep quality, parental satisfaction.

RESULTS

GNB significantly prolonged time to first rescue analgesia vs control (median 240 vs 120 min; P<0.001). GNB reduced swallowing pain vs both comparators throughout 24h (P≤0.008). Post-operative nausea: 6.7% GNB vs 20% RI vs 33.3% control (P=0.036). Parental satisfaction was significantly higher in the GNB group. No major anesthetic complications (arrhythmias, bronchospasm) in any arm.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study (Iran); operator-dependent skill required for GNB; 3-arm allocation limits statistical power per pairwise comparison; follow-up limited to 24 hours; generalizability to different anesthetic protocols uncertain.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Bilateral GNB with ropivacaine outperforms peritonsillar infiltration and sham for post-tonsillectomy pain—especially swallowing comfort—through 24 hours. ENT surgeons or anesthesiologists comfortable with the technique should consider adding GNB to multimodal analgesia protocols for pediatric day-case tonsillectomy.

Why score 4: Double-blind 3-arm RCT (n=90) directly comparing GNB vs peritonsillar ropivacaine infiltration vs sham for pediatric tonsillectomy pain. GNB demonstrated superior analgesia, prolonged time to first rescue analgesic, and lower nausea without significant additional risk. Directly actionable for ENT surgeons performing pediatric tonsillectomies.

Risk Factors, Symptom Burden, and Treatment Response in Pediatric REM-Predominant OSA: Evidence From the Childhood Adenotonsillectomy Trial.

Wang C, Wang A, Wu J et al. … Wang Y (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology)

BACKGROUND

REM-predominant OSA (REM-AHI/NREM-AHI ratio ≥2) may be a biologically distinct pediatric OSA subtype. Whether this distinction predicts differential response to adenotonsillectomy (AT) vs watchful waiting (WW) is unknown, as current guidelines do not differentiate management by OSA subtype.

METHODS

Post-hoc secondary analysis of the Childhood Adenotonsillectomy Trial (CHAT), a landmark multicenter RCT of 382 children aged 5–9.9 years with PSG-confirmed OSA randomized to AT or WW. REM-predominant OSA defined as REM-AHI:NREM-AHI ≥2. Primary outcome: OSA resolution at 7 months. Multivariate logistic regression used.

RESULTS

55.8% of children had REM-predominant OSA; enriched in Black children and those with obesity. REM-predominant OSA was associated with greater daytime sleepiness, higher AHI, and lower oxygen saturation nadirs. Children with REM-predominant OSA randomized to WW were significantly less likely to achieve spontaneous resolution (aOR 0.52, 95% CI 0.28–0.97). In the AT arm, OSA resolution was similar between subtypes (aOR 0.75, NS).

LIMITATIONS

Post-hoc secondary analysis with associated multiplicity concerns; WW arm received sleep hygiene education which may have confounded outcomes; single 7-month follow-up point; generalizability to non-CHAT populations uncertain.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Children with REM-predominant OSA—especially Black children and those with obesity—are significantly less likely to spontaneously improve with watchful waiting than those with non-REM-predominant OSA. Surgeons should consider this subtype identification when discussing WW vs AT, and may prioritize earlier surgical intervention for children with REM-predominant disease.

Why score 4: Well-powered (n=382) secondary analysis of the landmark CHAT RCT demonstrating that REM-predominant OSA—present in 55.8% of children—is associated with significantly lower spontaneous resolution with watchful waiting (aOR 0.52), while adenotonsillectomy resolves both subtypes equally. Directly informs surgical decision-making in pediatric sleep surgery.

Effects of NDURE on Timely Adjuvant Radiotherapy in HNSCC: Subgroup Analysis of an RCT.

Hughes JM, Kistner-Griffin E, DeMass R et al. … Graboyes EM (senior, Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery)

BACKGROUND

Delays in initiating postoperative radiation therapy (PORT) after surgery for locally advanced HNSCC are associated with worse oncologic outcomes and disproportionately affect underserved populations. Enhanced patient navigation programs may reduce these disparities.

METHODS

Exploratory, post-hoc subgroup analysis of a single-center RCT (NCT04030130) at MUSC. 145 adults with locally advanced HNSCC undergoing surgery + PORT randomized 1:1 to NDURE (navigation-based care coordination) vs usual care. Primary outcome: timely PORT initiation (≤6 weeks). Subgroups analyzed included age, race, partnership status, AJCC stage, reconstruction type, and surgical complications.

RESULTS

NDURE improved timely PORT initiation across nearly all subgroups: age ≥65 (RD +43%, 95% CI 19–64%), Black patients (RD +46%, 95% CI 10–73%), non-partnered (RD +32%), AJCC Stage IV (RD +39%), free flap reconstruction (RD +40%), and postoperative complication (RD +39%). Only one subgroup (White patients) showed non-significant benefit.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study; exploratory post-hoc subgroup design with limited power per subgroup; MUSC patient demographics may not generalize; navigation program specifics are institution-dependent.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

The NDURE navigation intervention broadly improves timely PORT initiation in HNSCC patients across demographic and clinical risk strata—including patients at highest risk of treatment delay. Head and neck cancer programs should consider structured navigation as a standard component of post-surgical care coordination, particularly for elderly, Black, socially isolated, or medically complex patients.

Why score 4: Subgroup analysis of a completed RCT published in The Laryngoscope showing that the NDURE navigation intervention improved timely PORT (≤6 weeks) across nearly all demographic and clinical subgroups in locally advanced HNSCC, including populations at highest risk of treatment delays. Actionable for HNC multidisciplinary teams concerned with equity and outcomes.

Total Parotidectomy Versus Less-Than-Total Parotidectomy for Early-Stage Low Grade Parotid Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Choi N, Lee HR, Seok J et al. … Lee GJ (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery Sungkyunkwan University )

BACKGROUND

Total parotidectomy (TP) has historically been advocated for parotid malignancies to ensure tumor clearance and sampling of intraparotid lymph nodes. However, less-than-total parotidectomy (LTT) may offer comparable oncologic control with superior functional outcomes by reducing facial nerve manipulation.

METHODS

PRISMA-guided systematic review and meta-analysis. Four retrospective cohort studies of T1-2 low-grade parotid cancer were included. Primary outcomes: overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS). Pooled hazard ratios computed using random-effects models.

RESULTS

No significant difference in OS between LTT and TP across 3 studies. RFS was comparable across all 4 studies. Heterogeneity was negligible for both OS (I²≈0%) and RFS. No evidence of publication bias detected.

LIMITATIONS

Only 4 retrospective studies, all with high risk of bias; selection bias possible (lower-stage tumors may have preferentially received LTT); lacks standardized functional outcome reporting; cannot draw causal conclusions.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For T1-2 low-grade parotid malignancies confined to the superficial lobe, LTT appears oncologically equivalent to total parotidectomy based on current retrospective evidence. A margin-focused, function-preserving surgical approach is defensible; prospective studies with standardized functional and oncologic reporting are needed to confirm this recommendation.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis of 4 retrospective cohort studies demonstrating no significant difference in OS or RFS between total parotidectomy and less-than-total parotidectomy for T1-2 low-grade parotid cancer. Evidence supports margin-focused, function-preserving surgery as oncologically safe in this population.
