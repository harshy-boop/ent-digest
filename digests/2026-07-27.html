Postoperative Oral Dexamethasone and Pediatric Tonsillectomy Morbidity: A Randomized Clinical Trial

Shaffer AD, Omar M, Maguire RC, Dohar JE, Stapleton AL … Chi DH (senior, Division of Pediatric Otolaryngology, UPMC Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh)

BACKGROUND

Tonsillectomy is one of the most painful otolaryngologic procedures in children. It was unclear whether a short postoperative oral steroid course could reduce post-adenotonsillectomy (AT) pain and decrease opioid use.

METHODS

Quadruple-blinded parallel RCT at a single tertiary academic center (UPMC Children's Hospital). 209 children aged 3–17 years randomized to oral dexamethasone 0.5 mg/kg (max 20 mg) or placebo on postoperative days 2, 4, and 6. Primary outcome: mean pain score before analgesics on days 2–8. Secondary outcomes: opioid prescriptions, ER visits, hemorrhage, return to normal diet.

RESULTS

Mean pain on days 2–8 was 0.72 points lower in the dexamethasone group (4.12 vs 4.84) but wide 95% CIs precluded definitive conclusions on the primary endpoint. The dexamethasone group had significantly fewer opioid prescriptions (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.06–0.84) and ER visits for pain (OR 0.12, 95% CI 0.003–0.91). Differences in hemorrhage, readmission, and return to diet were clinically negligible.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center design; high attrition in pain diary completion (131 of 209 completed diaries) limits precision of the primary outcome. Wide confidence intervals on secondary outcomes require cautious interpretation.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

A 3-dose post-AT oral dexamethasone regimen (days 2, 4, 6) is safe and associated with meaningful reductions in opioid prescriptions and ER revisits — an important opioid-stewardship benefit. Consider adopting as routine practice in pediatric AT, pending replication in a larger multicenter trial.

Why score 4: Quadruple-blinded RCT (n=209) in JAMA Otolaryngol from UPMC. Demonstrates that a 3-dose oral dexamethasone course after pediatric adenotonsillectomy significantly reduces opioid prescriptions (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.06–0.84) and ER visits for pain (OR 0.12, 95% CI 0.003–0.91), with no increase in posttonsillectomy hemorrhage. Modest but consistent pain reduction on days 12–13 post-op. Practice-implication: supports routine post-AT steroid course as opioid-sparing adjunct.

Cisplatin-Induced Hearing Loss Prevention With Intratympanic Therapy: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Kons ZA, Kersbergen CJ, Goss D … Remenschneider AK (senior, Department of Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery, Massachusetts Eye and Ear)

BACKGROUND

Cisplatin-induced hearing loss (CIHL) is a common irreversible complication of cisplatin-based chemotherapy. Intratympanic (IT) injections of cytoprotective agents have been trialed in humans. No prior meta-analysis has consolidated the evidence.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis of OVID Medline, Embase, Web of Science, and Cochrane. Inclusion: prospective randomized trials evaluating IT agents for CIHL prevention. Ten studies (284 patients) were identified, evaluating IT dexamethasone, IT N-acetylcysteine (NAC), and IT sodium thiosulfate (STS). Random-effects models used to pool audiometric outcomes.

RESULTS

Pooled analysis across all agents and frequencies showed no significant difference in hearing thresholds between treatment and control ears (prediction interval −3.77 to +3.20 dB). Subgroup analyses of IT dexamethasone and IT NAC also were non-significant. A single STS trial showed a significant reduction in ASHA-defined ototoxicity (40% vs 85%, p=0.003) but STS data were not amenable to pooling.

LIMITATIONS

Only 10 trials with heterogeneous dosing, timing, and audiometric endpoints. Small total sample size (284 patients). Single promising STS result requires replication. Systemic STS (FDA-approved as Pedmarqsi) is not addressed in this analysis.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

IT dexamethasone and IT NAC should not be routinely used for CIHL prevention — evidence does not support them. IT STS warrants further investigation. For pediatric patients, systemic STS (FDA-approved) remains the validated option. Counsel patients that no proven IT prophylaxis exists and enroll eligible patients in trials.

Why score 4: Rigorous meta-analysis of 10 prospective RCTs (n=284) from Mass Eye & Ear. No intratympanic agent (dexamethasone, N-acetylcysteine) showed a consistent benefit for preventing cisplatin-induced hearing loss. IT sodium thiosulfate showed a signal (40% vs 85% ASHA ototoxicity, p=0.003) in one trial but data were not amenable to pooled analysis. A high-value negative result that should guide ototoxicity counseling and trial design.

Efficacy of Endoscopic Interventions for Subglottic Stenosis in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Alqutub A, Mogharbel A, Awadh MA, Alsulami OA, Alamoudi HA … Al-Khatib T (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery, King Abdulaziz University, Jeddah)

BACKGROUND

Subglottic stenosis (SGS) in adults may arise from prior intubation, autoimmune disease (especially GPA), or idiopathic causes. Multiple endoscopic modalities exist (CO2 laser, balloon dilation, microdebrider, mitomycin-C adjuvant) but comparative efficacy is unclear.

METHODS

PRISMA-compliant systematic review of 4 databases from inception to August 2024. Included studies evaluating endoscopic interventions for adult SGS. 55 studies (3,091 patients) met inclusion criteria. Primary outcome: success rate. Secondary outcomes: recurrence, decannulation rate, complications, need for open surgery.

RESULTS

Overall endoscopic success rate was 72.9%. Recurrence was common. CO2 laser-based procedures trended toward higher success, but heterogeneity across studies (different disease severity, definitions of success, and follow-up duration) precluded definitive head-to-head comparisons. Complication rates were generally low.

LIMITATIONS

Very high heterogeneity across included studies. Lack of standardized definitions for 'success,' disease severity grading, and follow-up duration. Most studies were retrospective case series with no control group.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Endoscopic management of adult SGS is effective in approximately three-quarters of cases, but recurrence is the norm rather than the exception. No single endoscopic modality has proven definitively superior. Surgeons should document disease severity (Cotton-Myer grade), duration of follow-up, and standardized outcomes to enable future comparative analysis. Open surgery should remain available for refractory cases.

Why score 4: Large meta-analysis (55 studies, n=3,091) providing the most comprehensive synthesis to date of endoscopic management of adult SGS. 72.9% overall success rate but high recurrence and extensive study heterogeneity. CO2 laser-based approaches trended toward higher success. Directly applicable to airway surgeons managing idiopathic or post-intubation SGS.

The Utility of Intranasal Corticosteroids in Association With Dupilumab for Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyposis: A Real-Life Multicenter Study

Orlando P, Licci G, Reale M, Seccia V, Giorli A … Gallo O (senior, Department of Experimental and Clinical Medicine, University of Florence, Florence, Italy)

BACKGROUND

Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα) has transformed management of severe CRSwNP. INCS are traditionally co-prescribed, but whether they add meaningful benefit on top of biologics — and whether patients need to maintain strict INCS adherence — remains uncertain.

METHODS

Multicenter real-world observational study across 7 ENT centers in Tuscany, Italy. 123 patients with severe uncontrolled type-2 CRSwNP on dupilumab 300 mg q2 weeks for 12 months. Outcomes: SNOT-22, nasal polyp score (NPS), olfaction (SSIT-16), Lund-Mackay CT score (LMS). INCS adherence classified as complete vs non-adherent (≥1 missed prescription).

RESULTS

23.8% of patients were non-adherent to INCS. Both groups showed significant improvement across all endpoints. Non-adherent patients had faster early SNOT-22 reduction; adherent patients showed better early NPS and SSIT improvements. By 12 months, no statistically significant differences remained between groups in any outcome measure.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective, non-randomized design with self-reported adherence. No structured INCS adherence monitoring. Non-adherence likely underreported. Future prospective RCTs needed to confirm these findings.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

In CRSwNP patients on dupilumab, strict INCS adherence may not be essential for equivalent 12-month disease control. While INCS may accelerate early improvement in polyp scores and olfaction, the long-term outcomes are similar with or without adherent INCS use. This finding may reduce patient burden and simplify regimens, but should be confirmed in a randomized trial before formal guideline changes.

Why score 4: Real-world 7-center Tuscany cohort (n=123) addressing whether INCS is necessary alongside dupilumab for CRSwNP. INCS non-adherence (24% of patients) did not result in worse 12-month SNOT-22, nasal polyp score, or olfactory outcomes. Clinically important: suggests INCS may not be obligatory in patients on dupilumab for severe type-2 CRSwNP — relevant to the rapidly growing biologics-prescribing ENT community.

Outcomes in Orbit-Sparing Versus Orbit-Sacrificing Surgery for Sinonasal Malignancies With Orbital Involvement: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Tan CJ, Yeo BSY, Warrier M, Chan JH, Png LH … Teo NWY (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery, Singapore General Hospital)

BACKGROUND

Sinonasal malignancies with orbital involvement historically required orbital exenteration (orbit-sacrificing surgery, OcS) for oncologic control. However, selected patients may undergo orbit-preserving surgery (OrPS) to maintain visual function. Prior evidence has been inconsistent and largely from single-institution retrospective series.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis of PubMed, Embase, and Scopus from inception to June 2024. Included longitudinal studies comparing OrPS vs OcS in sinonasal malignancies with orbital involvement. 12 studies (758 patients) included. Primary outcomes: 5-year overall survival (OS) and 5-year recurrence-free survival (RFS). Evidence quality assessed by Newcastle-Ottawa Scale and GRADE.

RESULTS

5-year OS was comparable between OrPS (55%, 95% CI 0.32–0.76) and OcS (53%, 95% CI 0.34–0.70). 5-year RFS was significantly higher in OrPS patients (64%, 95% CI 0.44–0.80) vs OcS (48%, 95% CI 0.13–0.84). Functional outcomes in orbit-preserved patients were described as good in descriptive reviews.

LIMITATIONS

High degree of selection bias inherent in retrospective studies — patients chosen for OrPS typically have less extensive orbital invasion. Heterogeneity in tumor histology (squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, etc.), staging systems, and adjuvant treatment. Quality of evidence by GRADE is low to moderate.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For selected sinonasal malignancies with limited orbital involvement, orbital preservation is oncologically safe and provides better recurrence-free survival without sacrificing overall survival — possibly because OcS patients have higher tumor burden at baseline. Multidisciplinary team evaluation (skull base surgery, ophthalmology, radiation oncology) should guide individualized orbital preservation decisions. Exenteration should be reserved for cases with full orbital fat invasion or unresectable orbital apex involvement.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis of 12 studies (758 patients) directly addressing one of the most debated questions in sinonasal oncology: when is it oncologically safe to spare the orbit? Orbit-preserving surgery showed comparable 5-year OS (55% vs 53%) and significantly better 5-year recurrence-free survival (64% vs 48%) compared to exenteration, with preserved functional eye. Supports the current trend toward orbital preservation in carefully selected patients.

Systematic review of standalone voice therapy (without botulinum toxin) for spasmodic dysphonia. Available evidence is limited to small, mostly uncontrolled series; no RCTs exist.
