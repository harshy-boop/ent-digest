Exercise-Induced Laryngeal Obstruction: A Randomized Controlled Trial of Surgical Treatment

Sandvik L, Helland SV, Kvidaland HK et al. … Heimdal JH (senior, Institute of Clinical Medicine, University of Bergen, Bergen, Norway)

BACKGROUND

Exercise-induced laryngeal obstruction (EILO) causes exertional dyspnea especially in adolescents and young adults. Surgical options—supraglottoplasty—are used but lacked RCT-level evidence. This trial was designed to fill that gap.

METHODS

Forty-one patients with symptomatic supraglottic EILO refractory to conservative management were randomized to full supraglottoplasty (FSP; aryepiglottic fold incisions including cuneiform tubercle tip), minimally invasive supraglottoplasty (MISP; six laser punctures), or wait-and-see control. Continuous laryngoscopy during exercise (CLE) was performed before and after, grading obstruction at glottic and supraglottic levels at moderate and maximal intensities. Patient-reported outcomes (PROM) also collected.

RESULTS

FSP was superior to control for supraglottic CLE scores at moderate intensity (p=0.036) and for both glottic and supraglottic CLE scores at maximum intensity (p=0.047 and p<0.001). FSP was superior to MISP for supraglottic CLE at maximum intensity (p<0.001). MISP did not differ from control on any CLE endpoint. Both FSP and MISP reduced breathing-related PROM during activity vs. baseline, but only FSP improved general breathing difficulty vs. controls (p<0.001) and vs. MISP (p=0.042).

LIMITATIONS

Small sample (n=41); single-center design limits generalizability. PubMed pub-types do not yet include 'Randomized Controlled Trial' indexing. Long-term follow-up not reported. MISP PROM benefit without objective CLE improvement raises questions about mechanism.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Full supraglottoplasty (FSP) is the evidence-based surgical choice for refractory supraglottic EILO, improving both objective laryngoscopic scores and patient-reported outcomes. MISP provides subjective benefit but does not improve CLE outcomes; its role remains uncertain. Surgeons should select FSP when offering surgery for supraglottic EILO.

Why score 5: First RCT of surgical treatment for EILO. Three-arm design (FSP vs. MISP vs. control) with objective CLE outcome measure. Demonstrates FSP superiority over both MISP and observation on laryngoscopic and patient-reported outcomes; directly practice-changing for surgeons considering supraglottoplasty.

Isolated Adenoidectomy for Pediatric Sleep-Disordered Breathing: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis

Smaily H, Benchekroun S, Francis E et al. … Sirois C (senior, Centre of Excellence on Aging of Quebec (CEVQ), CIUSSS de la Capitale-Nationale, Quebec, QC, Canada)

BACKGROUND

Adenotonsillectomy is standard for pediatric sleep-disordered breathing (SDB), but exposes children to tonsillectomy-associated morbidity. Isolated adenoidectomy may suffice in selected patients, particularly younger or smaller children, but aggregate evidence was lacking.

METHODS

Systematic review of MEDLINE, EMBASE, and CENTRAL (inception–March 2025) per PRISMA. Included 22 observational studies reporting isolated adenoidectomy outcomes for SDB/UAO in children (weighted mean age 5.2 years; total n=4,460). Meta-proportion analysis for symptomatic improvement and reoperation rate; subgroup analyses by age.

RESULTS

Mean AHI reduction was 11.3 events/h (4 studies). Pooled symptomatic improvement: 75% (95% CI 60–88%). Pooled further surgical intervention rate: 17% (95% CI 11–24%). Subgroup analyses showed greater improvement and lower reoperation in children >3 years.

LIMITATIONS

All 22 included studies are observational; no randomized controlled trial data available. Significant heterogeneity anticipated across studies. Publication bias unexcluded. Specific tonsil-grading thresholds for patient selection not standardized across studies.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Isolated adenoidectomy achieves symptomatic improvement in approximately 75% of appropriately selected children with SDB, with a 17% reoperation rate. It is a valid, tonsillectomy-sparing first surgical step, particularly in children >3 years or with adenoid-predominant obstruction. Definitive RCTs are still needed to formalize selection criteria.

Why score 4: PRISMA-compliant meta-analysis of 22 studies (4,460 children) in the flagship AAO-HNS journal quantifying isolated adenoidectomy efficacy for pediatric SDB. Provides pooled estimates guiding patient selection for a conservative first surgical step before adenotonsillectomy.

Impact of Dose and Administration Schedule on Biologic Efficacy in Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps: A Network Meta-analysis

Khalifa MA, Ahmed Shimal A, Mahmoud MH et al. … Irhayyif SR (senior, College of Medicine, University of Baghdad, Baghdad, Iraq)

BACKGROUND

Dupilumab, omalizumab, mepolizumab, and benralizumab are approved for CRSwNP but have never been directly compared in head-to-head RCTs. Dosing schedules vary and their relative effects on different clinical endpoints are unclear.

METHODS

Frequentist network meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (4,861 adults with CRSwNP) evaluating biologic agents plus placebo across all approved dosing regimens. Primary outcomes: SNOT-22 (QoL), Nasal Polyp Score (NPS, objective burden), and UPSIT (olfaction). Interventions ranked by P-score.

RESULTS

Dupilumab 600 mg loading/300 mg q1–2 weeks ranked highest for SNOT-22 QoL improvement (P-score 0.907) and olfactory recovery (SMD 1.91, 95% CI 1.24–2.59; P-score 0.006). Benralizumab 30 mg q4 weeks ranked highest for objective polyp burden reduction (P-score 0.864). Substantial heterogeneity and global inconsistency detected in SNOT-22 network.

LIMITATIONS

Indirect comparisons only; no head-to-head RCTs. Significant heterogeneity limits confidence in point estimates. Dosing arms may have different baseline characteristics. Senior author affiliation is from a resource-limited setting, potentially limiting access to some biologics in original trials.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Biologic selection for CRSwNP should be outcome-driven: choose dupilumab when the priority is quality of life and olfactory recovery; consider intensive benralizumab dosing when objective polyp shrinkage is the primary goal. No single agent dominates across all endpoints, reinforcing the need for individualized shared decision-making.

Why score 4: Network meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (4,861 patients) provides the highest-level indirect comparative evidence for biologic selection in CRSwNP. Directly actionable: dupilumab leads for QoL and olfaction; intensive benralizumab leads for polyp burden. This nuance is immediately useful at biologics-MDT meetings.

A Multicenter Retrospective Study on Severe Complications of Acute Otitis Media and Sinusitis in Healthy Children in the Context of COVID Pandemic (CAOS-kids)

Andorfer KE, Grønseth T, Butt JZ et al. … Renner K (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology, University Hospital Regensburg, 93053 Regensburg, Germany)

BACKGROUND

Severe intracranial complications of pediatric sinusitis and AOM (e.g., epidural abscess, meningitis, sigmoid sinus thrombosis) and high-grade orbital complications are rare but life-threatening. Multiple centers reported a perceived post-pandemic increase and this study was designed to quantify it internationally.

METHODS

Multicenter retrospective case series at 8 centers across 7 countries (Europe and North America), capturing 289 cases from 2012 to 2023. Cases per semiannual period were compared pre- vs. post-pandemic. Age, diagnosis, treatment, and microbiological data collected. Acute COVID-19 association evaluated.

RESULTS

Cases per semiannual period increased from 6 to 28 for sinogenic and 3 to 12 for otogenic complications—approximately 4.7× and 4× increases respectively. Surgical interventions per patient rose slightly in the sinogenic cohort. No association with acute COVID-19 infection was identified; the increase began after lifting of pandemic restrictions.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective design with referral bias at tertiary centers. Case identification dependent on local ICD coding. Causal mechanism (immune debt hypothesis) remains unproven. Long study period may include diagnostic and coding changes over time.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Severe pediatric ENT complications from AOM and sinusitis surged approximately 4× after pandemic restrictions lifted, likely due to immune debt from reduced respiratory viral exposure during lockdowns. In the post-pandemic era, maintain a low threshold for CT imaging and early surgical intervention in children presenting with worsening sinusitis or AOM, particularly when signs of orbital or intracranial involvement are present.

Why score 4: International 8-center dataset from 7 countries confirming a significant post-pandemic surge in the most feared pediatric ENT complications—intracranial sinogenic/otogenic complications and high-grade orbital cellulitis. Quantifies the magnitude of increase (~4.5× for sinogenic cases) and informs clinical vigilance in the ongoing post-pandemic period.

Hemithyroidectomy Eligibility Under ATA 2015 and 2025 Criteria: A Reclassification Cohort Study

Ergenç M, Metin P, Demirbağ B, Uğurlu MÜ … Uğurlu MÜ (senior, Breast and Endocrine Surgery Unit, Marmara University School of Medicine, Istanbul, Turkey)

BACKGROUND

The ATA published updated differentiated thyroid carcinoma guidelines in 2025, modifying risk stratification for hemithyroidectomy eligibility and completion thyroidectomy indications. The magnitude of surgical practice change these updates imply in a real-world cohort was unknown.

METHODS

Retrospective cohort of 98 hemithyroidectomy patients for differentiated thyroid carcinoma (January 2015–November 2025) at a tertiary center. After excluding NIFTP and tumors of uncertain malignant potential, 86 cases were reclassified under both ATA 2015 and ATA 2025 criteria using final histopathology. McNemar test for paired comparison.

RESULTS

Hemithyroidectomy eligibility increased from 60.5% (ATA 2015) to 81.4% (ATA 2025), an absolute increase of 20.9 percentage points (95% CI 12.3–29.5; p<0.001). Completion thyroidectomy indications fell from 44.2% to 29.1% (reduction 15.1 points; 95% CI 7.5–22.7; p<0.001). Actual completion thyroidectomy rate (40.7%) exceeded guideline-based projections, suggesting additional clinical factors drive real-world decisions.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center; retrospective hindsight reclassification (could not inform preoperative decisions). Small cohort (n=86). Long-term oncologic outcomes not assessed. Generalizability limited to centers with similar case mix and histopathological expertise.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

ATA 2025 criteria substantially de-escalate thyroid cancer surgery: 1 in 5 additional patients is now eligible for hemithyroidectomy, and completion thyroidectomy indications shrink by ~15%. Surgeons and endocrinologists should update their preoperative counseling to reflect ATA 2025 risk stratification. Real-world completion rates still exceed guideline thresholds, highlighting a need for institutional review of decision-making practices.

Why score 4: First real-world reclassification study comparing ATA 2015 vs. 2025 surgical eligibility criteria in the same cohort. Demonstrates a 20.9-point increase in hemithyroidectomy eligibility and 15.1-point decrease in completion thyroidectomy indications under ATA 2025—directly quantifying the practice de-escalation that the new guidelines imply for thyroid surgeons and endocrinologists.
