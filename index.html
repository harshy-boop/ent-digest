Orforglipron for Obstructive Sleep Apnea: Results From 2 Randomized Clinical Trials (ATTAIN-OSA).

Malhotra A, Gottlieb DJ, Woehrle H et al. … ATTAIN-OSA Investigators

BACKGROUND

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) affects >1 billion adults and is strongly associated with obesity and cardiometabolic disease. Existing treatments are mechanical (CPAP, oral appliances, surgery) and do not address the underlying obesity. Orforglipron is a novel oral, non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist in development for weight management and type 2 diabetes.

METHODS

ATTAIN-OSA comprised two 52-week, Phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs at 66 sites across the US, Argentina, Brazil, China, Czech Republic, Germany, Japan, Mexico, and Taiwan. Trial 1 enrolled 363 adults with moderate-to-severe OSA (mean AHI 50.7 events/h) not on PAP; Trial 2 enrolled 349 adults (mean AHI 57.6 events/h) on PAP. BMI ≥27 required. Patients received once-daily orforglipron (up to 17.2-mg tablet equivalent) or placebo for 52 weeks. Primary endpoint: change in AHI at week 52.

RESULTS

In Trial 1, mean AHI change was −17.8 events/h with orforglipron vs −4.5 with placebo (difference −13.2; 95% CI −17.7 to −8.7; p<0.001). In Trial 2, −17.3 vs −5.3 (difference −12.0; 95% CI −17.1 to −6.9; p<0.001). Six of 7 key secondary endpoints (including sleep-specific hypoxic burden, patient-reported sleep impairment, and hsCRP) were statistically significant, favoring orforglipron. GI adverse events occurred in 68% of orforglipron vs 37% placebo patients in Trial 1.

LIMITATIONS

Trial 2 patients were already CPAP-users, blurring the mechanistic interpretation. The trials were conducted over only 52 weeks — durability beyond one year and effects of discontinuation are unknown. The predominantly male, obese cohort may limit generalizability to non-obese OSA patients. The GI adverse event rate (68%) is clinically meaningful and may affect real-world adherence.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Orforglipron, taken once daily orally, reduces AHI by ~13 events/h in both CPAP-naïve and CPAP-using patients with moderate-severe OSA and obesity. This is the first Phase 3 RCT evidence for a pharmacologic agent achieving clinically meaningful AHI reduction. ENT and sleep surgery clinicians should anticipate patients asking about this option; it is likely to reshape the first-line conversation for OSA in obese patients, potentially reducing surgical referrals or serving as a bridge/adjunct to existing treatments.

Why score 5: Two Phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCTs at 66 sites in 9 countries published in JAMA. Orforglipron produced a 13.2 events/h reduction in AHI vs placebo (p<0.001) in both CPAP-naïve and CPAP-using cohorts; 6 of 7 secondary endpoints were significant. First oral GLP-1 RA Phase 3 evidence in OSA — practice-changing for sleep surgery counseling.

Perioperative Camrelizumab Plus Chemotherapy in Locally Advanced Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma: A Randomized Clinical Trial.

Liu F, Zhang Y, Ma H et al. … He Y (senior, Department of Oral Maxillofacial-Head and Neck Oncology, Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, College of Stomatology, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, National Center for Stomatology, National Clinical Research Center for Oral Diseases, Shanghai Key Laboratory of Stomatology, Shanghai Research Institute of Stomatology, Shanghai, China.)

BACKGROUND

Resectable locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) has poor long-term outcomes despite multimodal therapy including surgery and adjuvant chemo-radiotherapy. Neoadjuvant immune checkpoint inhibition could prime the tumor microenvironment and reduce distant recurrence, but Phase 3 evidence was lacking.

METHODS

CAMORAL was a Phase 2 multicenter RCT at 4 academic hospitals in China (Oct 2020–Mar 2024). Adults aged 18–75 with untreated, resectable stage III–IVB HNSCC (82% oral cavity primary) were randomized 1:1: neoadjuvant camrelizumab 200 mg + nab-paclitaxel + carboplatin (2 cycles) → surgery → risk-adapted adjuvant therapy + maintenance camrelizumab (up to 15 cycles); vs upfront surgery → standard adjuvant therapy. Primary endpoint: 2-year event-free survival (EFS).

RESULTS

2-year EFS was 77.8% in the neoadjuvant-adjuvant arm vs 46.1% in controls (HR 0.34; 95% CI 0.17–0.68; p=0.001). Major pathological response (MPR): 47.6%; pathological complete response (pCR): 25.4%; vs 0% in controls. 2-year OS: 92.0% vs 69.1% (HR 0.25; 95% CI 0.10–0.64). Grade 3+ adverse events occurred in 79.4% of the treatment arm vs 56.5% controls; 1 fatal event was in the control arm.

LIMITATIONS

Phase 2 trial (n=125) with predominantly oral cavity primaries from a single country (China); generalizability to Western populations and other HNSCC subsites (oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx) is uncertain. The pCR endpoint is a surrogate; long-term OS data at 5 years are not yet available. The high toxicity rate (79% Grade 3+) will require careful patient selection.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Perioperative camrelizumab significantly improved 2-year EFS and OS vs surgery alone in resectable locally advanced HNSCC. For head and neck oncology surgeons, this Phase 2 signal — with a 25% pCR rate — supports the emerging perioperative immunotherapy paradigm and justifies awaiting Phase 3 confirmation (similar to KEYNOTE-689 for pembrolizumab). Multidisciplinary tumor boards should discuss neoadjuvant immunotherapy eligibility for high-risk resectable HNSCC cases.

Why score 4: Phase 2 multicenter RCT (CAMORAL) published in JAMA Oncology. Perioperative camrelizumab + chemotherapy nearly halved the hazard of disease recurrence vs surgery alone in resectable stage III–IVB HNSCC (HR 0.34; p=0.001), with pCR of 25.4% in the treatment arm vs 0% in controls. Limitations include single-country (China) recruitment and Phase 2 size (n=125); prospective Phase 3 trials needed.

Effect of orofacial myofunctional therapy with auto-monitoring on the apnea-hypopnea index in patients with mild-to-moderate obstructive sleep apnea: a multicenter randomized controlled trial.

Hansen DD, Tinbod U, Feng X et al. … Hrubos-Strøm H (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology, Akershus University Hospital, Sykehusveien 25, 1474, Lørenskog, Norway.)

BACKGROUND

Orofacial myofunctional therapy (OMT) — exercises targeting tongue position, lip seal, and pharyngeal muscle tone — has been proposed as a non-invasive alternative or adjunct treatment for OSA. Prior meta-analyses suggested benefit, but protocols varied widely and study quality was inconsistent.

METHODS

A multicenter RCT at academic sleep centers. Treatment-naive adults with mild-to-moderate OSA (confirmed by polysomnography) were randomized to 12 weeks of a standardized OMT program vs waitlist control (n=52 each). Three-night polysomnography was completed before and after the intervention. Electronic adherence monitoring was used. ITT analysis with linear mixed-effects models; per-protocol analysis also performed.

RESULTS

ITT analysis (n=104) showed no significant effect of OMT on AHI: mean group difference in AHI change was 0.6 events/h (95% CI −3.3 to 4.5; p=0.774). No significant changes were found in oxygen desaturation measures or Epworth Sleepiness Scale score. Mean adherence was 64.6%. Higher adherence was not associated with greater AHI reduction (β=−0.1; p=0.353).

LIMITATIONS

12-week duration may not be sufficient to achieve anatomical remodeling; some prior studies used 3–4 months. Waitlist control rather than sham therapy could introduce expectation bias. Patients had mild-moderate OSA only; severely affected patients or those with specific phenotypes (tongue-based obstruction) might respond differently. Multicenter design may introduce therapist variability despite standardized protocol.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This well-powered multicenter RCT finds that standardized OMT does not reduce AHI in treatment-naive adults with mild-to-moderate OSA, contradicting earlier meta-analyses that relied on lower-quality studies. Sleep surgeons and ENT clinicians can use this finding to counsel patients: OMT should not be recommended as monotherapy for AHI reduction in this population, though its role in adjunctive therapy (post-surgery, with CPAP) or in specific phenotypic subgroups remains to be tested.

Why score 4: Well-designed multicenter RCT (n=104) in JCSM. Standardized OMT showed no significant effect on AHI vs waitlist (difference 0.6 events/h; p=0.774) over 12 weeks, with no improvement in desaturation or sleepiness. Despite 65% adherence, the null result definitively challenges OMT's routine use as monotherapy for mild-moderate OSA and resolves conflicting prior evidence.

Does Montelukast Decrease Post-Adenotonsillectomy Pain in Children? A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Redmann AJ, Buck LS, Furstein J, de Alarcon A … de Alarcon A (senior, Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, University of Cincinnati, Cincinnati, Ohio, USA.)

BACKGROUND

Opioid-sparing strategies after pediatric adenotonsillectomy (AT) are a priority given concerns about respiratory depression in children with OSA/SDB. Montelukast, a leukotriene receptor antagonist, has anti-inflammatory properties and has been informally used perioperatively for pain control despite limited evidence.

METHODS

A single-center, double-blind RCT at University of Cincinnati. 116 children aged 3–8 undergoing AT with 23-hour observation for SDB were randomized to perioperative montelukast vs placebo (1 preoperative dose + 1 postoperative dose in 92 patients). Primary outcomes: postoperative opioid use in PACU, FLACC pain scores, post-tonsillectomy hemorrhage (PTH), and postoperative contacts. Level of evidence: 2.

RESULTS

No significant differences between groups: opioid use in PACU 46% (control) vs 45% (montelukast; p=0.9); FLACC pain scores similar; PTH rates similar between groups. Number of postoperative contacts was not significantly different. The trial was adequately powered and groups were demographically balanced.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center study from one academic institution limits generalizability. The study did not include home pain diary outcomes or days-to-return-to-normal-diet, which are important patient-centered endpoints. The dosing regimen (perioperative only) may not capture montelukast's potential for a longer post-operative course. The trial was adequately powered for PACU opioid use but may be underpowered for PTH.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Montelukast does not reduce postoperative opioid use, pain scores, or complications after pediatric adenotonsillectomy for SDB compared to placebo. ENT surgeons who have been prescribing perioperative montelukast for its off-label analgesic properties should discontinue this practice. Focus should remain on validated multimodal analgesia strategies (acetaminophen, NSAIDs when appropriate, regional techniques) for post-AT pain management.

Why score 4: Double-blind RCT (n=113) in Laryngoscope from University of Cincinnati Otolaryngology. Montelukast showed no reduction in opioid use (46% placebo vs 45% montelukast, p=0.9), pain scores (FLACC), or post-tonsillectomy hemorrhage vs placebo in children 3–8 undergoing adenotonsillectomy for SDB. Closes a commonly-used off-label analgesic strategy.

Prevalence of Synchronous and Metachronous Malignancy in Sinonasal Inverted Papilloma: Meta-Analysis.

McHugh N, Bawa J, Qureishi A et al. … Mughal Z (senior, Department of Otolaryngology, John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford, UK.)

BACKGROUND

Sinonasal inverted papilloma (IP) is a benign but locally aggressive neoplasm with established potential for malignant transformation, most commonly to squamous cell carcinoma. Quoted malignancy rates in textbooks and clinical practice vary widely (3–17%), creating uncertainty in patient counseling and surveillance planning.

METHODS

PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis. Databases searched: Embase, MEDLINE, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library from 2005 to June 2026. Studies of histologically confirmed sinonasal IP with reported malignancy rates were included. Random-effects model used to pool prevalences. 68 studies comprising 7,412 patients were identified.

RESULTS

Pooled overall malignancy prevalence: 5.6% (95% CI 4.5–6.8%). Synchronous malignancy (carcinoma present at initial diagnosis): 4.3% (95% CI 3.4–5.3%). Metachronous malignancy (developing after initial treatment): 1.3% (95% CI 0.8–2.0%). Pooled recurrence rate (benign recurrence): 17.5% (95% CI 15.0–20.2%).

LIMITATIONS

High heterogeneity is expected in meta-analyses of prevalence data from multiple institutions and time periods. Surveillance protocols and biopsy practices vary across studies, potentially affecting detection of metachronous malignancy. Studies published before 2005 were excluded, so historical comparison is limited. Individual patient-level risk factors (site, HPV status, revision surgery) could not be analyzed.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

The pooled malignancy rate for sinonasal IP is approximately 5.6%, substantially lower than the 10–15% often cited in clinical discussions, with the synchronous rate (4.3%) driving most of the risk. Rhinologists and ENT surgeons can use these data for pre-operative counseling — the risk of harboring a concurrent carcinoma approaches 1 in 23 patients, justifying thorough pre-operative workup and careful intraoperative frozen-section assessment. The 1.3% metachronous rate reinforces the need for long-term post-resection surveillance.

Why score 4: PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis of 68 studies (n=7,412) in the Laryngoscope. Pooled malignancy prevalence of 5.6% (synchronous 4.3%, metachronous 1.3%) — lower than most commonly cited figures, with recurrence rate of 17.5%. Provides a methodologically robust benchmark for counseling and surveillance after endoscopic resection of sinonasal inverted papilloma.

A prospective cohort study in JAMA Otolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery analyzed latent class structures of CRS symptom phenotypes by disease duration in medically refractory CRS. A Preoperative Grading System for Endoscopic Sinus Surgery.

A JAMA Otolaryngology–Head & Neck Surgery study developed a preoperative grading system (CRS Severity Index) for endoscopic sinus surgery (ESS) that integrates patient-reported phenotype, symptom severity, and CT imaging into a single ordinal score. A retrospective multicenter cohort study using the Chang Gung Research Database (2002–2022) evaluated whether preoperative systemic inflammatory indices (NLR, PLR, and related markers) predict free flap complications in oral cavity cancer reconstruction. Unmasking early-morning hypoglycaemia with CGM in multifactorial vertigo: the interplay of recurrent BPPV, orthostatic dysregulation and hypnotic polypharmacy.

A BMJ Case Reports submission describes the unmasking of early-morning hypoglycemia using continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) in a patient presenting with multifactorial vertigo.
