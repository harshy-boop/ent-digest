Precision Phenotyping With Audiometric Data and Gene Discovery for Sensorineural Hearing Loss.

DeFreese AJ, Rubat du Mérac T, Sheng Q et al. … Jan TA (senior, Vanderbilt University, Nashville, TN)

BACKGROUND

Prior genome-wide association studies (GWAS) of sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) using ICD diagnostic codes have identified few reproducible loci, possibly because billing-code phenotyping captures only clinically documented cases rather than the full audiometric spectrum.

METHODS

Two parallel GWAS were conducted from a single biobank using whole-genome sequencing: one with ICD-9/10-coded SNHL (10,164 cases / 51,305 controls) and one with audiometric pure-tone averages (PTA) as a continuous trait (n=16,057). Polygenic risk scores (PRS) from each approach were validated in the NIH All of Us cohort.

RESULTS

PTA-based GWAS identified 3 genome-wide significant loci mapping to 4 genes — EML6, SPTBN1, ARHGEF28, and EYA4 — with SNV-based heritability estimate of 11.78% (SE 2.84%). ICD-based GWAS identified no significant loci (h²=2.90%). PTA-derived PRS predicted self-reported deafness in All of Us (OR 1.09, 95% CI 1.07–1.12); ICD-derived PRS did not (OR 1.00, 95% CI 0.97–1.02). Genome-wide correlation between PTA GWAS and hearing aid use was r=0.985.

LIMITATIONS

Single tertiary biobank with predominantly European ancestry; PTA data from a clinic-based population may introduce ascertainment bias toward symptomatic individuals; modest heritability estimate (~12%) indicates most genetic architecture of SNHL remains undiscovered.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Audiometric PTA data captures ~4× the heritability of ICD-code–based phenotyping and successfully identifies known deafness genes (EYA4) missed by billing-code approaches. Researchers and audiologists building precision medicine or genetic counseling programs for hearing loss should use continuous audiometric phenotypes rather than diagnostic codes — this directly improves polygenic risk score utility in clinical and research contexts.

Why score 4: Well-designed comparative GWAS from Vanderbilt in the specialty flagship (JAMA Otolaryngol) demonstrates that audiometric phenotyping identifies known SNHL loci (EYA4) and meaningful PRS missed entirely by ICD-code methods; a methodological shift with direct implications for precision medicine in audiology.

Cryotherapy as Treatment for Chronic Rhinitis: Long-Term Results of a Prospective, Multicenter Registry.

Luong AU, Mehendale NH, Szabo E et al. … Yen DM (senior, General Otolaryngology, Rhinology, Bethlehem ENT, Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, USA.)

BACKGROUND

ClariFix cryotherapy targets posterior nasal nerve branches to reduce symptoms of chronic rhinitis. Prior randomized and single-center data supported short-term efficacy; long-term multicenter real-world evidence was lacking.

METHODS

Prospective, nonrandomized observational registry at 22 tertiary and private ENT practices. Adults with allergic or nonallergic chronic rhinitis received ClariFix treatment; outcomes (reflective Total Nasal Symptom Score [rTNSS], mini-RQLQ, PGIC, CGI) were assessed through 24-month follow-up.

RESULTS

354 participants (932 total treatments). Mean rTNSS change from baseline was significant at all follow-up points (P<0.001). ≥73% of participants were rTNSS responders (≥1-point reduction) and ≥74% were mini-RQLQ responders at all follow-ups. Clinician-rated improvement was 87.1% at 3 months. No device-related serious adverse events; adverse events were transient and resolved with OTC treatment. Patients with baseline rTNSS ≥7 showed greater benefit.

LIMITATIONS

Nonrandomized observational design; no sham arm limits causal attribution; high responder threshold (≥1 rTNSS point) may inflate response rates; predominantly tertiary practice enrollment may limit generalizability to community settings.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

ClariFix cryotherapy delivers durable (≥24 months) symptomatic improvement in chronic rhinitis across diverse practice settings, with >73% response rates and an excellent safety profile. Patients with higher baseline symptom burden (rTNSS ≥7) benefit most. This largest long-term dataset confirms ClariFix as a viable office-based option in chronic rhinitis treatment algorithms.

Why score 4: Largest prospective multicenter registry for ClariFix cryotherapy in the specialty flagship (Otolaryngol HNS); 24-month follow-up in 354 diverse patients confirms durable benefit and excellent safety; directly supports clinical integration into chronic rhinitis management.

Longitudinal associations of obstructive sleep apnea with clinical disorders relevant to school functioning in school-aged children: A multicenter cohort study.

Chang YP, Wang JJ, Tsai CY et al. … Tseng JJ (senior, National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University / Taichung Veterans General Hospital, Taiwan)

BACKGROUND

Pediatric obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is known to impair neurocognitive function, but whether it translates into formally diagnosed clinical disorders during longitudinal care was less established, and most studies relied on research-based cognitive assessments rather than real-world clinical records.

METHODS

Multicenter retrospective cohort: children aged 6–12 years with polysomnography-confirmed OSA (no adenotonsillectomy [AT]) were propensity-score matched to healthy controls (n=7,136 pairs). Hazard ratios for four disorder domains — learning, speech/language, emotional, anxiety — were estimated over 1, 2, 3, and 5 years. Secondary analysis examined children who subsequently underwent AT.

RESULTS

OSA without AT was associated at 5 years with HR 1.86 (95% CI 1.56–2.23) for learning disorders, HR 2.25 (1.82–2.77) for speech and language disorders, HR 1.45 (1.04–2.04) for childhood-onset emotional disorders, and HR 1.41 (1.26–1.56) for anxiety disorders. Children who subsequently underwent AT showed more favorable 5-year patterns across all domains.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective administrative data; disorder ascertainment via clinical coding may undercount milder presentations; AT group is confounded by indication; no PSG follow-up to confirm OSA resolution after surgery.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

PSG-confirmed pediatric OSA left untreated carries nearly 2× the risk of learning disorders and 2.25× the risk of speech/language disorders over 5 years in this large propensity-matched cohort. The more favorable trajectories in children who received AT underscore the clinical urgency of early surgical intervention for pediatric OSA.

Why score 4: Largest propensity-matched multicenter cohort linking untreated PSG-confirmed pediatric OSA to real-world clinical diagnoses across four school-functioning domains (n=7,136 pairs, 5-year follow-up); HRs of 1.4–2.3 provide the strongest longitudinal evidence yet for early surgical intervention in pediatric OSA.

Radiofrequency Ablation for Low-Risk T1N0M0 Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Lee MK, Ha EJ, Lee JY et al. … Kim JH (senior, Seoul National University Hospital, Seoul, Republic of Korea)

BACKGROUND

Ultrasound-guided radiofrequency ablation (RFA) is increasingly offered as an alternative to surgery for low-risk T1N0M0 papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC), but individual study sizes and heterogeneous follow-up limited confidence in pooled estimates of efficacy, safety, and volume regression.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 35 studies (9,787 RFA-treated T1N0M0 PTC tumors) from MEDLINE, EMBASE, Cochrane Library, and KoreaMed. Random-effects models estimated pooled complete disappearance, persistent tumor, disease progression, complication rates, and volume-reduction ratio (VRR) at sequential time points. Subgroup analyses compared T1a vs T1b outcomes.

RESULTS

Pooled complete disappearance rate: 87.2% (I²=97.9%); persistent tumor: 0.1%; disease progression: 1.3% (newly developed 0.8%, LN metastasis 0.2%); VRR: 63.4% at 6 mo → 88.7% at 12 mo → 97.9% at 24 mo; overall complication rate: 2.7%; major complication: 0.02%. T1b tumors had significantly lower complete disappearance (58.8% vs 89.2%) and higher persistent tumor rates than T1a tumors.

LIMITATIONS

Very high heterogeneity (I²=97.9% for complete disappearance); predominantly Asian center data that may not generalize; variable follow-up lengths and definitions; no head-to-head RFA vs. surgery RCT included; T1b subgroup differences must be interpreted cautiously.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

RFA achieves ~87% complete disappearance for T1a PTC with an excellent safety profile (<3% complications, 0.02% major). T1b lesions respond substantially less well. These pooled data support offering RFA as a credible surgical alternative for appropriately selected T1a PTCs, and highlight the need for enhanced surveillance and patient counseling when considering RFA for T1b disease.

Why score 4: Largest meta-analysis of RFA for T1N0M0 PTC (35 studies, 9,787 tumors) with comprehensive efficacy and safety data through 24 months; T1a vs T1b subgroup difference is clinically actionable and will directly shape shared decision-making between thyroid surgeons and their patients considering ablation.

Primary Tumor Location and Lymphatic Spread in Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma.

Fournier I, Zafereo ME, Hamidi S et al. … Maniakas A (senior, MD Anderson Cancer Center, Houston, TX)

BACKGROUND

Standard teaching holds that superior-pole papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) preferentially spreads to upper cervical nodes and inferior-pole tumors to central/lower levels, informing the extent of neck dissection. This assumption had been tested only in small series.

METHODS

Single-center retrospective cohort at MD Anderson (1999–2023): 1,688 patients with PTC undergoing primary thyroid surgery with central (99%) and/or lateral (45%) neck dissection. Tumor location (superior/mid/inferior pole, isthmus) confirmed by ultrasound and pathology. Multivariable logistic regression for nodal positivity by tumor location.

RESULTS

Central neck positivity was similar across all tumor locations: superior 79%, inferior 73%, mid 74%, isthmic 76% (P=0.22); MVR showed no significant differences. Among 45% undergoing lateral neck dissection: Level III was most involved (81%), Level IV (75%), IIa (60%), Vb (36%), IIb (9%). Superior-pole tumors had significantly higher ipsilateral Level III involvement vs. inferior and mid-pole (P<0.01), but the absolute rate remained high across all groups.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective single-center design at a quaternary referral center with case-mix bias toward higher-risk disease; only 45% of cohort underwent lateral neck dissection (selection bias); study does not address microscopic or subclinical skip metastases.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Primary tumor location in PTC does not meaningfully predict nodal distribution and should not drive the extent of neck dissection. Level III is uniformly at high risk and should be included in all therapeutic lateral neck dissections for PTC, regardless of tumor pole. This 24-year MD Anderson dataset provides strong evidence to retire tumor-location–based dissection planning.

Why score 4: Largest single-institution dataset (n=1,688, MD Anderson, 24-year span) directly testing and disproving current surgical doctrine that PTC tumor location guides neck dissection extent; the finding that Level III is at universal high risk has immediate and practice-altering implications for head and neck oncologic surgeons.

Shaving Versus Resection on Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve in Thyroid Cancer Surgeries: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Meta-analysis of 13 retrospective cohorts (532 patients) comparing RLN shaving vs resection in thyroid cancer found that shaving was associated with lower recurrence (p=0.007), better vocal fold mobility (p<0.0001), and longer maximum phonation time (p=0.04) with no overall survival difference. The authors caution that shaved patients had less advanced RLN invasion, so these results likely reflect selection bias rather than a true treatment effect. Standardized grading of RLN invasion (Shin classification) and prospective study designs are needed before conclusions about superiority can be drawn. Relevant for surgeons counseling patients with RLN-abutting or -invading differentiated thyroid cancer about functional and oncologic tradeoffs.

3 Systematic Review
Voice Outcomes Following Thermal Ablation for Thyroid Nodules: A Systematic Review.

Systematic review of 18 studies (5,168 patients) examining voice outcomes after thermal ablation (RFA, MWA, laser, HIFU) of thyroid nodules found a pooled post-procedural vocal cord palsy rate of 1.4%, with permanent VCP in only 0.05% (2 cases). However, pre-procedure laryngeal assessment using nasoendoscopy or imaging was documented in only ~40% of patients, suggesting the true rate may be underestimated due to unrecognized baseline dysfunction. Subjective voice symptoms without objective cord dysfunction were generally mild and self-limiting. Routine objective laryngeal evaluation before and after thyroid ablation is essential for accurate patient counseling and detection of VCP complications.

3 Meta-Analysis
Mohs vs. Wide Local Excision for Early-Stage (AJCC 0-II) Cutaneous Head and Neck Melanoma: A Meta-Analysis.

Meta-analysis of 23 studies (7,090 cases) comparing Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) vs wide local excision (WLE) for early-stage (AJCC 0–II) cutaneous head and neck melanoma found consistently lower local recurrence with MMS (MIS: 1.7% vs 6.3%; Stage I–II: 1.3% vs 8.8%), less upstaging, and fewer secondary excisions, though no between-group difference reached statistical significance. The results support consideration of MMS for appropriately selected patients, particularly those with melanoma in situ in the head and neck where tissue preservation is surgically critical. High inter-study heterogeneity and retrospective designs limit firm conclusions. A prospective trial comparing MMS and WLE for head and neck melanoma would be valuable.

3 Retrospective Cohort
Nighttime Respiratory Disturbance and Hypoxemia Associated with Impaired Linear Growth in Children with Obstructive Sleep Apnea.

Large retrospective cross-sectional study (n=2,443 children) from West China Hospital found that both higher AHI and greater nocturnal hypoxemia (T90%) were independently associated with lower height z-scores and higher odds of subclinical growth impairment (height z-score ≤-1 SD) in children with OSA. Importantly, hypoxemia severity (T90%) showed stronger associations with height impairment than respiratory disturbance alone, suggesting that oxygen desaturation drives growth effects beyond mere arousal frequency. This is among the first studies to delineate independent contributions of hypoxia vs respiratory disturbance to linear growth in pediatric OSA. These findings support routine height monitoring in children presenting with OSA.

3 Retrospective Cohort
Efficacy of Tympanic Membrane Regeneration Therapy for Secondary Cholesteatoma With Tympanic Membrane Perforation.

Retrospective comparison of tympanic membrane regeneration therapy (TMRT) using basic FGF (bFGF)-soaked gelatin sponge vs conventional tympanoplasty for secondary cholesteatoma with TM perforation (TMRT: 41 ears; CTP: 33 ears) found 100% vs 93.9% TM closure, shorter operative time (65 vs 125 min), better hearing improvement (12.2 vs 7.5 dB), and lower cholesteatoma recurrence at 3+ years (2.4% vs 9.1%) with TMRT. The TMRT approach uses endoscopic cholesteatoma removal with subsequent bFGF-gelatin sponge application repeated up to 4 times without traditional flap harvest. Small retrospective single-center design and absence of randomization limit generalizability, but the data are intriguing for expanding the application of bFGF-based TM repair beyond simple perforations. Longer follow-up in larger cohorts is needed to confirm durability of cholesteatoma control.

3 Population-Based Study
Impact of Neighborhood Socioeconomic Status on Presentation and Treatment of Thyroid Cancer.

Population-based study of 88,894 patients with differentiated thyroid cancer in the California Cancer Registry (1988–2020) found that lower neighborhood socioeconomic status (nSES) was independently associated with higher odds of distant metastases at presentation (OR Q1 vs Q5=1.33) and longer time from diagnosis to treatment (OR=1.24). Hispanic, Filipino, and Chinese American patients had significantly higher odds of presenting with advanced disease compared with non-Hispanic White patients. These disparities likely reflect barriers to early diagnosis rather than tumor biology differences. The findings support targeted screening and improved access to care for lower-SES and minority patients with thyroid cancer.

3 Retrospective Cohort
Probe-Based Near-Infrared Autofluorescence for Parathyroid Identification During Thyroid Surgery: Clinical Outcomes, Surgeon Confidence, and Autofluorescence Thresholds.

Retrospective study at Massachusetts Eye and Ear (Harvard) compared probe-based near-infrared autofluorescence (NIRAF; PTeye) for intraoperative parathyroid identification during total thyroidectomy (n=84) vs historical visual-identification controls (n=103). PTeye-assisted cases had significantly fewer PTGs incidentally identified on final histology (4.8% vs 16.5%, p=0.018) and slightly higher postoperative calcium, though rates of inadvertent parathyroidectomy, autotransplantation, transient hypoparathyroidism, and permanent hypoparathyroidism did not differ. A modified autofluorescence ratio cutoff of 2.5 (vs the manufacturer's 1.2) improved specificity. PTeye provides useful adjunctive confirmation of parathyroid tissue, especially for glands identified with low visual confidence.

3 Retrospective Cohort
Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyposis in EGPA: A Real-Life Multidimensional Analysis Through Mepolizumab Dosing and Role of Adjunctive Endoscopic Sinus Surgery (ESS).

Retrospective analysis of 52 EGPA patients on mepolizumab (24-month follow-up) from the San Raffaele Hospital found that while systemic remission occurred in >90% and eosinophil counts fell from 700 to 59.5 cells/µL, NPS and LKS scores remained largely stable (polyps persisted in 64%, mucosal edema in 92%). ESS was required in 28.8% of patients despite biologic therapy, and sinonasal severity did not correlate with systemic BVAS or eosinophil counts. Continuous 100 mg mepolizumab showed the greatest endoscopic improvement. These findings confirm that sinonasal disease in EGPA may follow a partially independent inflammatory course, supporting combined biologic–surgical strategies for patients with persistent structural disease.

3 Prospective Cohort
Video Laryngeal Mask Airway for Visual Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Monitoring in Thyroid Surgery.

Prospective single-arm feasibility study (n=104) of a video flexible laryngeal mask airway (VFLMA) for simultaneous ventilation and direct visual RLN monitoring during thyroid surgery achieved successful ventilation with monitoring in 94.2% of patients. Among 137 monitored RLNs, all 7 loss-of-signal events correlated with transient postoperative vocal fold palsy (100% sensitivity), though confidence intervals were wide due to small event numbers. Transient ventilatory disturbances occurred in 21%, but resolved with simple measures; only 1.9% required intraoperative endotracheal conversion. This hypothesis-generating feasibility study requires comparative validation against standard EMG IONM before routine adoption.

3 Cross-Sectional Study
Implications of Site-Neutral Rate-Setting Methods for Medicare Spending.

Cross-sectional analysis from Johns Hopkins modeled two approaches to Medicare site-neutral payment for 57 APC groups across hospital outpatient departments (HOPDs), finding that both the PFS-equivalent and discounted-OPPS approaches would generate ~58–60% savings vs current OPPS rates. Across 23 APC categories, 10 had higher PFS-equivalent than discounted-OPPS payments, meaning a blanket discount approach would underpay for some services relative to physician-office rates. ENT procedures frequently performed in office or ASC settings (endoscopy, ablation, biopsies) are directly affected as site-neutral expansion shifts the economic calculus of practice setting. As policymakers expand site-neutral policy, service-specific payment comparisons matter — uniform discount approaches may inadvertently underpay for clinically intensive outpatient procedures.

3 Translational Study
mTORC1 as a Therapeutic Target to Mitigate Development of Airway Stenosis in Response to Injury.

Mouse model and translational human tissue study from USC Keck demonstrated that mTORC1 activity in tracheal mesenchyme is required for injury-induced tracheal stenosis: genetic Rptor ablation in tracheal mesenchyme completely prevented stenosis formation after mucosal injury. Early (but not late) rapamycin treatment also significantly reduced injury-induced tracheal stenosis severity; human tracheal stenosis tissues showed elevated pS6K (mTORC1 activation) compared to controls. These findings identify a narrow post-injury therapeutic window in which rapamycin-class drugs may prevent laryngotracheal stenosis. Translational applicability in patients with post-intubation subglottic/tracheal stenosis remains to be tested in clinical studies.

First-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus placebo plus chemotherapy in unresectable advanced/metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: minimum 3-year survival follow-up of a randomized phase 3 trial (RATIONALE-306).

Three-year follow-up of the RATIONALE-306 phase III RCT (649 patients) confirmed durable OS benefit of first-line tislelizumab + chemotherapy vs placebo + chemotherapy for advanced/metastatic esophageal squamous cell carcinoma: median OS 17.2 vs 10.6 months (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.59–0.80); 15.0% vs 2.9% progression-free at 3 years. Time to deterioration of physical functioning was not reached with tislelizumab vs 18.8 months with placebo, reflecting a clinically meaningful QoL preservation signal. No new safety signals emerged with extended follow-up. While esophageal SCC management is largely driven by medical oncology, these 3-year durability data are relevant for head and neck tumor boards managing patients at the esophageal interface.

3 Retrospective Cohort
Clinical Characteristics and Hearing Outcomes of Otitis Media With ANCA-Associated Vasculitis: A Four-Group Comparison Including Unclassified Disease.

Retrospective study of 56 patients (89 ears) with otitis media in ANCA-associated vasculitis (OMAAV) at a tertiary university hospital compared outcomes across four disease subgroups (EGPA, GPA, MPA, and unclassified). Unclassified OMAAV patients had significantly longer diagnostic delays and more severe hearing loss at presentation, yet responded comparably to other groups after immunosuppressive therapy — underscoring that early diagnosis is critical for hearing preservation. In GPA patients, otologic relapse did not consistently precede systemic relapse, arguing against using ENT status alone as a proxy for systemic disease activity. Otolaryngologists should maintain a low threshold for ANCA testing in adults with persistent bilateral middle ear effusion unresponsive to conventional treatment.

Treatment Persistence and Factors Associated With Discontinuation in Real-World Biologic Therapy for Chronic Rhinosinusitis With Nasal Polyps.

Retrospective single-center study of 95 CRSwNP patients initiating biologic therapy (dupilumab 88.4%, mepolizumab, omalizumab) in Tel Aviv between 2021 and 2025 analyzed real-world treatment persistence and reasons for discontinuation.
