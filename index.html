Phase 3 RCTThyroid Oncology

Efficacy and safety of dabrafenib plus trametinib in adults with differentiated thyroid cancer: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial.

Gao M, Park YJ, Lin CC, Krzyzanowska MK, Li Z, et al. … Wirth LJ (senior, Massachusetts General Hospital, Boston, MA)

BACKGROUND

BRAF V600E mutations occur in ~40–50% of papillary thyroid cancers. Patients with locally advanced or metastatic radioactive iodine-refractory (RAI-R) BRAF-mutated differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) have poor prognosis on standard multikinase inhibitors. Preliminary studies of the BRAF/MEK inhibitor combination dabrafenib + trametinib suggested clinical benefit warranting a definitive phase 3 trial.

METHODS

Global phase 3, randomized (2:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=153). Adults ≥18 years with previously treated, locally advanced or metastatic, RAI-R BRAF V600E-mutant DTC. Dabrafenib 150 mg twice daily + trametinib 2 mg daily (n=101) vs placebo (n=52). Primary endpoint: progression-free survival (PFS). Secondary: ORR, duration of response, overall survival.

RESULTS

Median PFS 12.8 months (95% CI 10.2–21.2) vs 3.7 months (95% CI 2.3–7.5); HR 0.38 (95% CI 0.25–0.57), p<0.0001. ORR: 57% (dabrafenib+trametinib) vs 4% (placebo); p<0.0001. Interim OS analysis did not yet reach significance (HR 0.66, p=0.083). Grade ≥3 AEs included pneumonia (8%), serous retinopathy (7%), and pyrexia (48% any grade). Serious AEs in 43% of treatment arm vs 25% placebo.

LIMITATIONS

Population predominantly Asian (86%), limiting generalizability to Western cohorts. OS data immature. High rate of serious AEs (43%), including rare grade 5 events. Serous retinopathy requires ophthalmology monitoring. Sponsor-funded trial (Novartis).

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Dabrafenib + trametinib significantly improves PFS and response in BRAF V600E-mutant RAI-refractory DTC and should be considered a standard option in this subgroup. Head and neck oncologists should ensure BRAF V600E mutational testing at diagnosis of advanced/metastatic DTC. Monitor for pyrexia, retinopathy, and cutaneous toxicity during therapy.

Why score 5: Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT published in The Lancet Oncology. Near-doubling of ORR (57% vs 4%) and PFS improvement (12.8 vs 3.7 months, HR 0.38) in BRAF V600E-mutant RAI-refractory thyroid cancer. Practice-defining evidence for a targetable oncogene in an otherwise difficult-to-treat metastatic subgroup. Senior author is Lori Wirth MD, MGH.

Retrospective CohortSleep Surgery / OSA

Hypoglossal Nerve Stimulation and the Incidence of Cardiovascular Disease.

Kondamuri N, Hyman MJ, Cai Y, LoSavio PS, Skolarus TA … Skolarus TA (senior, University of Chicago, Chicago, IL)

BACKGROUND

HGNS (hypoglossal nerve stimulation) is rapidly growing as an OSA treatment, but whether surgical treatment of OSA with HGNS reduces cardiovascular risk beyond AHI suppression was unknown. Prior CPAP trials have mixed results on CV event reduction.

METHODS

Retrospective cohort using Merative MarketScan commercial database. Adults undergoing HGNS 2015–2024 (n=3,786) matched on propensity scores to OSA controls meeting HGNS candidacy criteria (nonadherent to CPAP) who did not undergo implant (n=3,395). Time-varying covariate Cox model; surgery effect assumed to begin after 2 years post-implant.

RESULTS

After 2 years: HGNS associated with lower hazard of new-onset diabetes (HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.09–0.38) and hypertension (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.33–0.73). Among patients with baseline CVD: HGNS paradoxically associated with higher early CV event hazard (minor HR 1.44; major HR 1.62 within 2 years), but trending protective after 2 years (minor HR 0.42; major HR 0.40). Benefits accumulated with time.

LIMITATIONS

Retrospective, administrative claims — no AHI, no PAP adherence data, no OSA severity metrics available for matching. The early paradox (higher CV events in first 2 years in those with baseline CVD) likely reflects selection bias or immediate peri-operative burden, not a causal harm signal.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This is the first large cohort-level signal that HGNS may reduce new-onset cardiovascular disease after ≥2 years, supporting a longer-term metabolic benefit narrative. Counsel patients that CV risk reduction is a gradual benefit of sustained sleep treatment. RCT-level confirmation is still needed.

Why score 4: Large propensity-matched commercial insurance cohort (N=3,786 HGNS patients) in JAMA Otolaryngology with clinically important time-stratified cardiovascular outcomes data. First large evidence on whether HGNS carries downstream cardiovascular benefit, directly relevant to counseling the growing population of implant candidates.

Phase 2 RCTRhinology / Biologics

Dupilumab in chronic rhinosinusitis without nasal polyps: randomized phase 2 trial (ORION).

Lee SE, Peters AT, Gevaert P, Han JK, Bachert C, et al. … Phadke NA (senior, Sanofi, Cambridge, MA)

BACKGROUND

Dupilumab (anti-IL-4Rα monoclonal antibody) is FDA-approved for CRS with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), where type 2 inflammatory mechanisms are established. Whether dupilumab benefits CRS without polyps (CRSsNP) — especially in patients with elevated eosinophils — was unknown. ORION (NCT04678856) is the first phase 2 RCT specifically designed for this question.

METHODS

Multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 RCT. 71 adults with severe uncontrolled CRSsNP randomized 1:1 to dupilumab vs placebo for 24–52 weeks. Primary analysis: patients with serum blood eosinophil count (sBEC) ≥300 cells/μL (dupilumab n=16). Primary endpoint: change from baseline in Lund-Mackay CT score at week 24. Secondary: sTSS, UPSIT olfaction score, SNOT-22.

RESULTS

In the sBEC ≥300 group, dupilumab reduced LMK-CT from baseline (mean −6.63; 95% CI −8.71 to −4.54). Mean difference vs placebo: −5.95 (95% CI −8.38 to −3.51) favoring dupilumab. SNOT-22 trended toward improvement (−14.71, CI crossing zero). UPSIT (olfaction) also trended toward improvement (+5.91). Safety was balanced between arms.

LIMITATIONS

Small sBEC ≥300 primary analysis population (n=16 dupilumab arm). Phase 2 signal-finding study — underpowered for secondary symptom endpoints. Senior author from Sanofi (sponsor); risk of reporting bias. Larger pivotal trial needed before practice change.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Dupilumab improves CT sinus disease in eosinophilic CRSsNP (sBEC ≥300) and warrants a full phase 3 trial. Rhinologists should phenotype CRSsNP patients with peripheral eosinophil counts — if the phase 3 ORION result is positive, this would substantially expand biologic candidacy to the non-polyp population.

Why score 4: First randomized phase 2 trial of dupilumab in CRS without nasal polyps (CRSsNP) — a population where biologic therapy has no established role. Rhinology journal, small but well-controlled; radiographic primary endpoint. Signals an eosinophilic phenotype (sBEC ≥300) that may respond, expanding biologic candidacy criteria.

Multicenter CohortPediatric ENT / RRP

Long-term predictors of recurrence in pediatric recurrent respiratory papillomatosis.

Arshad E, Arshad A, Shafique A, Iqbal J, Saadir M … Younus F (senior, Wah General Hospital, Wah, Pakistan)

BACKGROUND

Pediatric recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) is caused by HPV 6/11 (most common) and rarely 16/18, with high recurrence after surgical debridement. Existing risk stratification focuses on HPV type and anatomic extent; novel patient-level predictors remain poorly characterized.

METHODS

Multicenter prospective cohort at 5 high-volume ENT centers in Pakistan, 2014–2023. 629 pediatric patients (onset <18 years) with histologically confirmed RRP. Kaplan-Meier and multivariable Cox regression for time to first recurrence requiring surgery. Inter-rater reliability kappa=0.78. Adjuvant therapy included cidofovir, interferon, and bevacizumab.

RESULTS

Recurrence in 65% pediatric vs 46% adult-onset (p<0.001). Independent predictors: HPV types 11, 16, 18; multiple lesions; tracheal involvement; young onset age; rapid lesion growth; low SES; immunodeficiency; short prior surgery intervals. Adjuvant HR: cidofovir 0.62, interferon 0.66, bevacizumab 0.53. Surgery type did not independently predict recurrence. SQOL correlated inversely with recurrence (r=−0.52, p<0.001).

LIMITATIONS

Single-country (Pakistan) cohort — resource-limited setting, referral center bias, and adjuvant therapy selection not randomized. No extended HPV genotyping beyond standard 4 types. Socioeconomic stratification may not translate to other health systems.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Risk-stratify pediatric RRP using HPV type, lesion growth velocity, tracheal involvement, and immunodeficiency status. Bevacizumab shows the strongest adjuvant association (HR 0.53) and warrants consideration in high-risk or rapidly recurring cases. Track SQOL as a clinically meaningful outcome endpoint.

Why score 4: Largest prospective multicenter cohort on pediatric RRP (n=629, 10-year follow-up). Identifies novel modifiable risk factors beyond HPV type, including lesion growth rate, SES, and immunodeficiency. Bevacizumab shows strongest adjuvant association. Provides the most comprehensive RRP recurrence risk model to date.

SR / Meta-AnalysisPediatric ENT / Otology

Balloon dilation of the eustachian tube in pediatric patients: A systematic review and meta-analysis of functional, audiological, and safety outcomes.

Saade R, Chkaiban Farah P, Elias L, Keyrouz E, Maalouf J … Matar M (senior, Lebanese American University Medical Center, Beirut, Lebanon)

BACKGROUND

Balloon dilation of the Eustachian tube (BDET) is FDA-approved for adults with ETD, but evidence in pediatric patients has been scattered across small, primarily retrospective case series. This SR/MA provides the first comprehensive pediatric-specific synthesis of functional, audiological, and safety outcomes.

METHODS

Systematic search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science (through October 2025). 20 studies included (18 retrospective, 2 prospective), 1,175 children (1,868 ears; mean age 9.1 years). Outcomes: ABG, PTA, ET scores, tubomanometry, Valsalva, tympanometry, TM findings, efficacy, and complications. Fixed- and random-effects meta-analysis.

RESULTS

Within-cohort: significant ABG improvement at 6 months (MD −14.84 dB; 95% CI −26.11 to −3.57) and 12 months (MD −10.60 dB). ET scores improved significantly (MD +3.78). Negative Valsalva results reduced (OR 17.21). However, comparative vs controls: no significant ABG difference (p=NS) and no significant efficacy/cure rate difference (OR 1.08; 95% CI 0.31–3.73). Pooled efficacy: 67%. Pooled complication rate: 3% (95% CI 2–4%).

LIMITATIONS

18 of 20 studies retrospective; high heterogeneity; most studies of fair quality. Only 3 comparative (controlled) studies. No pediatric-specific RCT yet exists. Senior author affiliated with institution without ENT academic prominence signals.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

BDET shows within-group audiological and functional improvements in pediatric ETD, with an acceptable 3% complication rate, but current comparative evidence does not confirm it outperforms ventilation tube insertion or watchful waiting. Consider in children with refractory ETD while awaiting pediatric RCT data. Do not routinely expand adult BDET protocols to children based on this evidence alone.

Why score 4: First SR/MA of BDET specifically in pediatric patients. 20 studies, 1,175 children (1,868 ears). Within-cohort benefits in ABG and ET scores are significant, but comparative evidence vs standard management does not yet confirm superiority. Critical synthesis for practices considering BDET expansion to pediatric populations.
