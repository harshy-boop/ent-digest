Bilateral Pacing Neuromuscular Prosthesis for Laryngeal Paralysis.

Powell ME, Li Y, Von Wahlde K … Zealear DL (senior, Vanderbilt University Medical Center, Nashville.)

BACKGROUND

Bilateral vocal fold paralysis causes severe dyspnea and often requires tracheotomy or laryngotracheal surgery. Unilateral reinnervation approaches have been explored, but bilateral restoration of abductor function remains a major unmet need. Neural pacing has been proposed as a reversible, function-restoring strategy.

METHODS

A bilateral neuromuscular prosthesis system is described that delivers coordinated electrical pacing to the posterior cricoarytenoid (PCA) muscles bilaterally, synchronized with the inspiratory phase of the respiratory cycle. The approach is detailed in this NEJM correspondence from the Vanderbilt group with prior experimental work in the field.

RESULTS

The prosthesis achieved coordinated bilateral abduction of the vocal folds timed to inspiration, restoring an adequate glottic aperture. Clinical and physiologic outcomes reported indicate functional airway improvement without requiring permanent tracheotomy in the case(s) described.

LIMITATIONS

Letter format limits full methodologic reporting; sample size is very small (case-level). Long-term device durability, infection risk, and electrode stability are not yet established. Independent replication is needed before widespread adoption.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

A bilateral PCA pacing prosthesis from the Vanderbilt laryngeal neural-stimulation group, published in NEJM, is the most important developmental step toward a device-based alternative to tracheotomy in bilateral laryngeal paralysis. Laryngologists managing bilateral vocal fold immobility should follow this technology closely.

Why score 4: NEJM publication — even as a letter — guarantees high editorial scrutiny. A bilateral pacing neuromuscular prosthesis for laryngeal paralysis addresses bilateral vocal fold immobility, a functionally devastating condition with limited satisfactory surgical solutions. Vanderbilt's Zealear DL has decades of neural-stimulation research in laryngeal physiology, lending credibility to the claim.

Testosterone Inhibition and Risk of Laryngeal and Hypopharyngeal Squamous Cell Carcinoma in Men.

Gottlieb-Vedi E, Santoni G, Hammarstedt-Nordenvall L … Lagergren J (senior, Upper Gastrointestinal Surgery, Department of Molecular Medicine and Surgery, Karolinska Institutet, Karolinska University Hospital, Stockholm, Sweden.)

BACKGROUND

Laryngeal and hypopharyngeal SCC occur predominantly in men, prompting the hypothesis that androgenic hormones (specifically dihydrotestosterone) may drive carcinogenesis. 5α-Reductase inhibitors (5-ARIs) block testosterone conversion to DHT and offer a pharmacological model to test this hypothesis in a real-world cohort.

METHODS

Population-based cohort study using the Swedish Prescribed Drugs and Health Cohort, covering July 2005–December 2022. Men prescribed 5-ARIs (finasteride, dutasteride) were compared to matched unexposed men. Incident laryngeal and hypopharyngeal SCC were ascertained through national cancer registries; Cox regression was used adjusting for smoking, alcohol, GERD, and socioeconomic factors.

RESULTS

5-ARI use was associated with a statistically significant reduction in risk of laryngeal and hypopharyngeal SCC. The association was consistent across subgroup analyses and strengthened with longer cumulative exposure, supporting a duration-response relationship and biological plausibility.

LIMITATIONS

Observational design — residual confounding by smoking severity and alcohol use cannot be fully excluded despite adjustment. Indication bias (men with BPH may differ systematically from unexposed men). Rare outcome limits statistical precision for subsite-specific analyses.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This large Swedish cohort adds epidemiological evidence that androgen signaling contributes to laryngeal and hypopharyngeal SCC risk in men. While not yet practice-changing in isolation, it supports incorporating androgen-axis status in future chemoprevention trials and warrants awareness when counseling men on 5-ARI treatment.

Why score 4: Large nationwide Swedish cohort (2005–2022) from the Karolinska group (Lagergren J) — a team with an established track record in upper GI and head-and-neck cancer epidemiology. 5α-Reductase inhibitors are widely prescribed; if they reduce laryngeal/hypopharyngeal SCC risk, the chemopreventive implications are directly actionable. Post-tonsillectomy Respiratory Complications in Children With Obesity: Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.

Kirkham EM, Raol N, Amin SC … Pattisapu P (senior, Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Nationwide Children's Hospital and the Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center, Columbus, Ohio, USA.)

BACKGROUND

Obesity increases the risk of upper airway compromise in the post-tonsillectomy period, but the magnitude of risk relative to non-obese children has not been quantified in a systematic synthesis. This affects triage decisions about same-day discharge versus overnight monitoring.

METHODS

Systematic review of Ovid MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, and Web of Science. Included studies reported post-tonsillectomy respiratory complications (oxygen desaturation, airway obstruction, respiratory failure) in patients <18 years. Meta-analysis using random-effects models; subgroup analyses by phase of care (PACU vs. inpatient) and indication (OSA vs. recurrent tonsillitis). Quality assessed with NHLBI tools.

RESULTS

Obese children had significantly higher odds of post-tonsillectomy respiratory complications than non-obese peers. The increased risk was present in both the PACU and inpatient phases. Subgroup analysis confirmed elevated risk regardless of surgical indication, with the largest effect sizes in children with comorbid OSA and obesity.

LIMITATIONS

Heterogeneity in obesity definitions and complication reporting across included studies. Most studies are retrospective; risk estimates may not account for unmeasured severity of OSA or anesthetic technique differences.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Obese children undergoing tonsillectomy carry a meaningfully higher risk of post-operative respiratory complications. Surgeons should incorporate this risk into discharge planning — strong consideration for overnight observation is warranted in obese children, particularly when OSA is also present.

Why score 4: SR/MA from Nationwide Children's Hospital quantifying post-tonsillectomy respiratory complication risk in obese children — a very common clinical scenario. Meta-analytic OR for inpatient respiratory events directly informs same-day surgery vs. admission decisions. Published in Otolaryngology–HNS, the flagship society journal.

Randomized Controlled Trial

Skill Transfer From 3D-Printed Temporal Bone Training to Cadaveric Mastoidectomy: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

Vallejos Riart SL, Marques da Silva Neto F, Koji Tsuji R … Ferreira Bento R (senior, Department of Otorhinolaryngology, University of São Paulo School of Medicine, São Paulo, Brazil.)

BACKGROUND

Mastoidectomy training relies heavily on cadaveric dissection, which is costly and access-limited. 3D-printed temporal bone simulators have been proposed as a scalable alternative, but evidence that simulator-acquired skills transfer to cadaveric performance was lacking.

METHODS

Prospective RCT at the temporal bone laboratory of a tertiary academic center. Thirty otolaryngology residents without prior mastoidectomy experience were randomized: intervention group received a 2-hour structured simulation on the Auris-Otobone 3D-printed model before cadaveric dissection; control group received standard theoretical instruction only. All residents then performed a simple mastoidectomy on cadaveric specimens.

RESULTS

Residents in the 3D-printed training group demonstrated significantly better mastoidectomy performance on cadaveric dissection compared to the control group. Objective scoring metrics showed improvement in both technical execution and time-to-completion, supporting positive skill transfer from the simulator to the cadaveric task.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center, small sample (n=30). Long-term retention of skills and transfer to actual patient cases were not assessed. The structured 2-hour session may not reflect typical unstructured use of simulation models.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This RCT provides level 1 evidence that precadaveric training on a 3D-printed temporal bone model improves mastoidectomy performance in novice residents. Programs with limited cadaveric access should consider incorporating structured 3D-printed simulation as a standard pre-cadaveric step.

Why score 4: RCT design is the highest evidence level in the surgical education literature. Testing a commercially available 3D-printed temporal bone model (Auris-Otobone) against standard instruction for mastoidectomy skill transfer directly addresses how residency programs can cost-effectively supplement cadaveric training. Published in Otology & Neurotology.

Active surveillance for a small low-risk papillary thyroid cancer: a 10-year prospective, observational study in an Italian tertiary care centre.

Molinaro E, Ghirri A, Prete A … Elisei R (senior, Department of Clinical and Experimental Medicine, Endocrine Unit, University of Pisa, Pisa, Italy.)

BACKGROUND

Active surveillance (AS) has been validated in Japanese cohorts for low-risk papillary thyroid microcarcinoma (mPTC ≤ 10 mm), but European prospective data with long-term follow-up were lacking. Western patients may differ in tumor biology, healthcare utilization, and the proportion choosing observation over immediate surgery.

METHODS

Prospective, single-center observational study at the University Hospital of Pisa (2014–2024). 260 adult patients with mPTC (≤13 mm) who voluntarily chose AS over immediate surgery. Surveillance consisted of periodic clinical evaluation and neck ultrasonography. Primary endpoints were tumor growth, lymph node metastasis, and crossover to surgery.

RESULTS

Over 10 years, the vast majority of patients on AS did not experience clinically significant tumor progression. The rate of conversion to surgery was low and attributable mostly to patient anxiety rather than disease progression. No patient developed distant metastasis or died from thyroid cancer during the observation period, confirming safety of AS in appropriately selected European patients.

LIMITATIONS

Single-center, non-randomized design; patients self-selected into AS. Longer-term outcomes beyond 10 years are unknown. Inclusion up to 13 mm (vs. 10 mm Japanese criterion) may slightly inflate surveillance cohort risk.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Ten-year European prospective data confirm that active surveillance is safe and feasible for low-risk papillary thyroid microcarcinoma. Thyroid surgeons and endocrinologists can counsel appropriately selected patients that observation is a valid alternative to immediate surgery, consistent with ATA low-risk guidelines and emerging multidisciplinary consensus.

Why score 4: First prospective European long-term (10-year) dataset on active surveillance for papillary thyroid microcarcinoma, from a well-respected University of Pisa endocrine oncology group (Elisei R). With the field debating surgery vs. observation for low-risk mPTC, 10-year prospective data directly informs shared decision-making. Published in BMJ Oncology.
