Perioperative respiratory adverse events in children undergoing adenotonsillectomy with laryngeal mask airway versus endotracheal tube ventilation: multicentre, randomised controlled trial.

Han Y, Yi X, Xia J et al. … Li W (senior, Department of Anaesthesiology, Eye and ENT Hospital, Fudan University, Xuhui District, Shanghai, 200031, China wenxian.li@fdeent.org.)

BACKGROUND

Airway management choice during pediatric adenotonsillectomy—laryngeal mask airway (LMA) vs endotracheal tube (ETT)—has been debated due to concerns about blood, debris, and airway control. Prior evidence came largely from small single-centre studies.

METHODS

Parallel-group RCT across 4 tertiary hospitals in China (2023-24). 400 children aged ≥2 to <7 years scheduled for adenotonsillectomy were randomised 1:1 to LMA (n=200) or ETT (n=200). Primary outcome: proportion with any perioperative respiratory adverse event (laryngospasm, bronchospasm, desaturation, obstruction, cough, or stridor).

RESULTS

22.5% (45/200) of the LMA group experienced PRAEs versus 57.5% (115/200) in the ETT group (absolute risk difference −35.0 pp, 95% CI −43.5 to −25.7; P<0.001). LMA outperformed ETT in both intraoperative and recovery-phase events. LMA-to-ETT conversion was necessary in 3 cases (1.5%).

LIMITATIONS

Single-country study performed by experienced anaesthesiologists at tertiary centres; results may not generalise to lower-volume or trainee-led settings. No long-term follow-up. Conversion events not counted as failures in primary analysis.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

In experienced hands, LMA significantly reduces perioperative respiratory complications versus ETT for pediatric adenotonsillectomy. Anaesthesiologists and ENT surgeons should discuss LMA adoption as a default approach when institutional expertise supports it.

Why score 5: Multicentre RCT in BMJ with N=400 and a large absolute risk reduction (−35 pp) in perioperative respiratory adverse events directly changes how experienced anaesthesiologists should approach airway management for pediatric adenotonsillectomy.

Efficacy of Intratympanic Sodium Thiosulfate Gel Injection to Prevent Cisplatin-Induced Ototoxicity.

Vittayakoonsakulchai K, Pitathawatchai P, Plodpai Y et al. … Prayuenyong P (senior, Department of Otolaryngology - Head and Neck Surgery, Faculty of Medicine, Prince of Songkla University, Hat Yai, Thailand.)

BACKGROUND

Cisplatin causes permanent ototoxicity, particularly at high and extended-high frequencies, in head and neck cancer patients. Intratympanic sodium thiosulfate (STS) gel has been proposed as a local otoprotectant to reduce this toxicity without compromising systemic anti-tumour efficacy.

METHODS

Single-blinded within-patient RCT; 28 adults with head and neck cancer receiving cisplatin. STS gel was injected into one ear within 3 hours before each cisplatin cycle; the contralateral ear served as the control. Audiometric assessments were performed at baseline and 1-3 months post-chemotherapy.

RESULTS

No significant difference in ASHA ototoxicity incidence between treated and control ears (p=0.683). However, treated ears showed significantly lower hearing threshold shifts at 4, 6, 8, 9, and 11 kHz (mean differences ~7-10 dB). Adverse effects across 74 injections: otalgia 10.8%, transient vertigo 10.8%, dizziness 14.8%, tympanic membrane perforation 4.0%.

LIMITATIONS

Small N=28 limits statistical power; single-blind design; within-patient control assumes symmetrical cisplatin ototoxicity risk, which may not hold; follow-up limited to 1-3 months post-chemotherapy; single centre.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Intratympanic STS gel does not significantly reduce overall ASHA-defined ototoxicity in this small trial, but preserves extended-high-frequency hearing in a clinically meaningful range. TM perforation (4%) is a real risk. Larger multicentre trials are needed before routine adoption in head and neck oncology practice.

Why score 4: Within-patient-controlled RCT in a flagship specialty journal addressing a practice-relevant problem in head and neck oncology. Despite not reaching significance on the ASHA ototoxicity primary endpoint, meaningful high-frequency hearing preservation (7-10 dB) with a generally safe profile warrants clinician attention.

Surgical Management of Pediatric Nodular Lymphocyte-Predominant Hodgkin's Lymphoma: A Systematic Review of Surgical Outcomes and Case Report.

Singh B, Raynor EM … Raynor EM (senior, Duke University Medical Center Durham North Carolina USA.)

BACKGROUND

Nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) accounts for ~5% of pediatric Hodgkin lymphoma. It presents with localized lymphadenopathy in boys aged 4-17 and has distinct pathology (CD20+/CD79a+). Long-term toxicity from chemotherapy and radiotherapy has motivated interest in surgical resection as a potentially curative approach for low-risk disease.

METHODS

Systematic review of PubMed and Scopus; 37 studies identified, 4 met inclusion criteria focusing on surgical outcomes. Authors also report their own Duke case of an 11-year-old who underwent modified neck dissection followed by chemotherapy for residual disease.

RESULTS

Across the 4 included studies, surgical resection yielded event-free survival rates of 57-77% and 100% overall survival in low-risk patients. Complete resection reduced recurrence risk; incomplete resections required adjuvant therapies. The case patient achieved remission with chemotherapy after incomplete resection and subsequent recurrence.

LIMITATIONS

Only 4 studies met inclusion criteria, all with heterogeneous populations and small sample sizes. This systematic review is severely limited by the rarity of the condition. No prospective comparative data exist; all evidence is observational.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Surgical resection is a viable primary treatment for carefully selected children with localized NLPHL when complete excision is achievable, potentially avoiding chemoradiation toxicity in low-risk patients. Head and neck surgeons should be aware of this multidisciplinary option; prospective registries are needed to build the evidence base.

Why score 4: Systematic review from Duke addressing a gap in pediatric head and neck oncology: whether surgery alone can spare selected children with localized NLPHL from long-term chemoradiation toxicity. Only 4 studies met criteria, but the 100% OS finding in low-risk patients is clinically notable.

An Exploratory Study of Image-Guided Cochlear Implant Intervention on Articulation and Phonological Processing in Children With Speech Sound Disorders.

An exploratory waitlist-deferred RCT from Vanderbilt (N=21) examined whether image-guided cochlear implant programming (IGCIP) improves articulation and phonological processing in children with CIs aged 4-13. Articulation deficits improved over 2 years relative to age norms, but phonological processing did not. IGCIP did not reach statistical significance for articulation in year 1, though children who received IGCIP within the first year showed clinically meaningful improvement compared with those who did not. Hypothesis-generating; a larger trial is needed to confirm.
