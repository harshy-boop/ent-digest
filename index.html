Endolymphatic Duct Blockage for Ménière's Disease: A Double-Blind, Randomised Controlled Trial

Schenck AA, van de Berg R, Peters TTA, Smulders YE … Smulders YE (senior, Dept. Otorhinolaryngology & Head and Neck Surgery, Leiden University Medical Centre, the Netherlands)

BACKGROUND

Ménière's disease treatment remains contentious. Endolymphatic duct blockage (EDB) was proposed as superior to the established endolymphatic sac decompression (ESD) for intractable disease, but no double-blind trial had been conducted.

METHODS

Double-blind multi-center RCT at 2 university and 5 non-university Dutch hospitals. 75 patients with definite unilateral Ménière's refractory to ≥2 corticosteroid injections were randomized intraoperatively to EDB (n=39) or ESD (n=36). Patients and investigators remained blinded for 12 months. Primary outcome: freedom from vertigo attacks (no attacks in the preceding 6 months at the 12-month visit).

RESULTS

59% EDB vs 67% ESD achieved the primary outcome (OR 0.72, 95% CI 0.28–1.84; p=0.65). No between-group differences in QOL, dizziness, tinnitus, or audiometric parameters. Three serious adverse events (one EDB, two ESD); no deaths. Higher baseline treatment expectations were the strongest predictor of success across both arms.

LIMITATIONS

Moderate sample (n=75; powered for a larger expected effect); neither arm included a sham-surgery control, so the absolute surgical benefit above natural history remains unknown. The expectation effect may mask lack of true therapeutic benefit for either procedure.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

EDB offers no advantage over ESD for refractory Ménière's disease. Do not preferentially adopt EDB. The strong expectation-outcome correlation raises the possibility that much of the perceived benefit from both procedures is non-specific. Future trials for intractable Ménière's surgery should incorporate a sham arm.

Why score 5: Double-blind, multi-center RCT in Lancet Regional — one of the few blinded surgical trials in otology. Negative primary outcome (EDB no better than ESD), which is itself practice-defining and overturns enthusiasm for the newer technique.

Dried Blood Spot Testing in Confirmed Congenital Cytomegalovirus Discovered by Expanded Targeted Testing

Kfoury P, Reddy M, Blackstad M, McVicar SB … McVicar SB (senior, Dept. Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, University of Utah School of Medicine, Salt Lake City)

BACKGROUND

Congenital CMV (cCMV) is the most common non-genetic cause of sensorineural hearing loss. Whether dried blood spot (DBS) universal screening or expanded targeted testing is preferable for identifying affected newborns is unresolved.

METHODS

Population-based cohort of 80 cCMV-positive newborns from 26 Utah birth hospitals identified by expanded targeted testing (2019–2024). Stored neonatal DBS samples underwent retrospective PCR (UL83 or IE gene) at the University of Minnesota, blinded to clinical status.

RESULTS

DBS PCR sensitivity was 86.3% (95% CI 77%–92%) overall; 92.7% (95% CI 80.6%–97.5%) for moderate-to-severe symptomatic cCMV; and 78.1% (95% CI 61.3%–89.0%) for mild symptomatic disease. Specificity 99.5%; PPV 100%; NPV 94.3%.

LIMITATIONS

Sample restricted to confirmed cCMV cases only — cannot directly model universal screening performance. Wide confidence intervals due to limited n=80. Asymptomatic infants (highest risk of missed delayed-onset hearing loss) are underrepresented.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Universal DBS screening would miss ~14% of cCMV cases overall — and ~22% of mild symptomatic cases most at risk for delayed-onset hearing loss. Pediatric otolaryngologists advocating for cCMV screening programs should understand that DBS alone is not a perfect substitute for expanded targeted testing, and newborns with hearing loss should undergo clinical CMV testing regardless of DBS results.

Why score 4: Prospective cohort in JAMA Otolaryngology addresses the sensitive clinical question of whether DBS universal screening would be adequate for cCMV — a leading preventable cause of sensorineural hearing loss. The 86% sensitivity gap and 78% sensitivity for mild disease directly inform screening policy.

Cost-Effectiveness of Immediate Septoplasty Versus Medical Management With the Option for Delayed Septoplasty for Nasal Airways Obstruction: A Multicentre, Open-Label, Randomised Controlled Trial

Homer T, Carrie S, Fouweather T, Teare D … Teare D (senior, Population Health Sciences Institute, Faculty of Medical Sciences, Newcastle University, Newcastle upon Tyne, UK)

BACKGROUND

The decision to proceed with immediate septoplasty vs. a trial of medical management in patients with symptomatic nasal obstruction from deviated septum lacks high-quality economic evidence.

METHODS

Economic evaluation alongside a 17-center, open-label UK RCT. Adults with NOSE score >30 randomized to immediate septoplasty (within 12 weeks) or 6-month medical management (nasal steroid + saline spray) with option for delayed surgery. Primary economic outcome: incremental cost per QALY gained from SF-36; model extrapolated to 24 months.

RESULTS

Immediate septoplasty was more costly (+£1,193) but more effective (+0.044 QALYs) at 12 months. Base-case ICER: £27,114/QALY; micro-costing ICER: £16,682/QALY. At 24 months, immediate septoplasty had a 99% probability of cost-effectiveness at the UK £20,000/QALY threshold.

LIMITATIONS

Open-label design; UK NHS cost context may not generalize to US payor environments. Recruitment 2018–2019 — cost structures have shifted. The 'delayed septoplasty' crossover option may underestimate the true cost difference.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For patients with moderate-to-severe nasal obstruction from septal deviation, immediate septoplasty is highly cost-effective over a 24-month horizon. This is the strongest economic evidence yet supporting a surgery-first approach, and can be used in pre-authorization discussions when insurers propose mandatory medical management trials.

Why score 4: Multi-center RCT economic analysis across 17 UK otolaryngology sites directly addresses the 'operate now vs. trial medical management' decision in rhinology. Findings at 24 months (99% probability cost-effective) support a surgery-first approach for moderate-to-severe symptomatic septal deviation.

Consensus Guidelines for Staging and Surveillance Imaging in Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma

Kassamali B, Schoenfeld JD, Sethi R, Bakst RL … Bakst RL (senior, Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard Medical School, Boston, Massachusetts)

BACKGROUND

Cutaneous SCC (CSCC) staging and surveillance imaging practices vary widely because NCCN recommendations are broad. No multidisciplinary consensus framework existed for when and how to image.

METHODS

45 experts (dermatology 33%, radiation oncology 20%, radiology 20%, medical oncology 16%, surgery 7%, otolaryngology 4%) completed 3-round Delphi surveys (Jan–Jun 2025). Consensus: ≥80% agreement; near-consensus: 70–79%.

RESULTS

Imaging recommended for CSCC with ≥15% estimated metastasis risk. Clear consensus for staging/surveillance in: bone invasion, invasion beyond subcutaneous fat, large-caliber perineural invasion, tumor ≥4 cm, or poorly-differentiated tumors ≥2 cm with LVI or PNI. CT preferred for nodal staging (84%) and surveillance (78%). Surveillance imaging recommended for at least 2–3 years.

LIMITATIONS

Expert consensus, not prospective outcomes data. Metastasis risk thresholds not prospectively validated. ENT representation was 4% of panel — head/neck-specific concerns may be underrepresented.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Head and neck surgeons co-managing high-risk CSCC now have clearer imaging criteria: stage with CT when tumor is ≥4 cm, deeply invasive, or poorly differentiated with additional risk factors, and maintain CT surveillance for 2–3 years. These recommendations should reduce under-imaging in high-risk patients and help standardize multidisciplinary care.

Why score 4: Multidisciplinary Delphi in JAMA Dermatology fills a genuine gap — CSCC imaging decisions have been highly variable. ENT/head & neck surgeons are involved in managing high-risk CSCC and these guidelines directly clarify when CT staging and surveillance are indicated.

Global Consensus on the Management of Primary Localized Chordoma

Radaelli S, Frezza AM, Fossati P, Baldi GG … Baldi GG (senior, International Sarcoma Working Group / Global Chordoma Consensus Group)

BACKGROUND

Chordoma is a rare malignant bone tumor affecting skull base, mobile spine, and sacrum, with 40–60% metastatic spread over the disease course. High-quality evidence is limited by rarity; the previous consensus was published in 2015.

METHODS

June 2025 Milan meeting of the Global Chordoma Consensus Group, involving experts across skull base surgery, spinal surgery, radiation oncology, pathology, radiology, and patient advocates. Literature review of 305 articles; levels of evidence and grades of recommendation assigned by anatomical site.

RESULTS

Core principle: treatment at experienced multidisciplinary centers is essential. Maximal safe surgery followed by high-dose, highly conformal radiotherapy (proton, carbon-ion, or photon SBRT) remains the central treatment strategy. Site-specific guidance provided for skull base, mobile spine, and sacrum. Updated recommendations for systemic therapy, long-term risk-adapted surveillance, and palliative/supportive care.

LIMITATIONS

Recommendations are largely expert opinion and retrospective data due to disease rarity. No Level 1 RCT evidence available. Treatment at specialized high-volume centers limits generalizability to community settings.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

ENT/skull base surgeons encountering clival or anterior skull base chordomas should adopt these 2025 guidelines: refer to experienced multidisciplinary centers, target maximal safe resection, and ensure high-dose conformal radiotherapy (proton preferred for skull base when available). The 2015 consensus is now superseded.

Why score 4: JAMA Oncology-published global consensus supersedes the 2015 guidelines for chordoma — a tumor ENT/skull base surgeons encounter at the clivus and anterior skull base. Updated guidance on surgical approaches, high-dose conformal RT, and systemic options directly affects subspecialty practice.
