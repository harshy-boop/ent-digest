Hearing aids for mild to moderate hearing loss in adults

Ferguson MA, Sarant JZ, Bennett RJ … Bothe E (senior, Curtin University, Perth, Australia)

BACKGROUND

Hearing aids are the mainstay treatment for mild-to-moderate hearing loss in adults. This 2026 update of the 2017 Cochrane review incorporates new RCTs and expands outcomes to include mental health and cognition.

METHODS

Cochrane Information Specialists searched CENTRAL, Cochrane ENT registry, MEDLINE, Embase, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, and ICTRP for published and unpublished RCTs. 2,261 participants across eligible trials were included. Risk of bias was assessed using Cochrane RoB 2 tool.

RESULTS

Hearing aids improved speech intelligibility, self-reported hearing handicap, and hearing-related quality of life versus no hearing aid (moderate-certainty evidence). New 2026 data show modest benefits on mental health measures and preliminary signals for cognitive benefit in adults with untreated mild-moderate hearing loss.

LIMITATIONS

Moderate certainty evidence overall; heterogeneity in hearing aid type, fitting protocols, and follow-up duration; cognitive outcomes remain preliminary with short follow-up periods in most RCTs.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

This Cochrane update reinforces that hearing aid prescription should be the standard of care for adults with mild-to-moderate hearing loss — including for mental health and possible cognitive benefits. ENT providers should counsel patients proactively on these broader benefits beyond speech clarity.

Why score 5: Updated Cochrane SR+MA (n=2,261, multiple RCTs) of the primary intervention for mild-to-moderate hearing loss, adding new outcomes (mental health, cognition). Cochrane methodology, landmark database update, and direct practice relevance earn a 5.

Radiofrequency Ablation Versus Cryoablation of the Posterior Nasal Nerve for Chronic Rhinitis: A Meta-Analysis and Systematic Review

Mose TA, Patel PC, Gelfond JAL … Chen PG (senior, University of Texas at San Antonio)

BACKGROUND

Posterior nasal nerve (PNN) ablation — by radiofrequency ablation (RFA) or cryoablation (CA) — is increasingly used for chronic rhinitis. Head-to-head comparisons have been limited.

METHODS

12 studies totaling 859 patients identified across 5 health science databases (January 2026). Meta-regression computed change in Total Nasal Symptom Score (TNSS), TNSS subscores, and RQLQ. Fisher's exact test compared adverse event (AE) rates.

RESULTS

Both modalities produced TNSS reductions surpassing the minimal clinically important difference (MCID). RFA and CA showed comparable overall efficacy on TNSS and RQLQ. No statistically significant difference in overall AE rates; RFA had higher minor procedural discomfort rates while CA showed a marginally higher rate of crusting in some analyses.

LIMITATIONS

Indirect (not head-to-head) comparison; heterogeneity across study populations, rhinitis phenotypes, and PNN ablation techniques; no blinded RCT comparing the two directly.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

RFA and cryoablation of the PNN are similarly efficacious for chronic rhinitis. Procedure selection can be guided by office setup, cost, and surgeon familiarity rather than expected efficacy differential. Both are appropriate options for patients with refractory rhinitis.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis (12 studies, n=859) in a specialty flagship journal comparing two widely used office procedures for chronic rhinitis. Results cross the MCID for TNSS and directly inform procedure selection for rhinologists.

Intracapsular tonsillectomy/tonsillotomy for severe pediatric obstructive sleep apnea: A PRISMA-based systematic review and exploratory meta-analytic synthesis

Torretta S, De Lucia G, Cozzi A … Battilocchi L (senior, Fondazione IRCCS Ca' Granda Ospedale Maggiore Policlinico, Milan, Italy)

BACKGROUND

Intracapsular tonsillectomy/tonsillotomy (IT) combined with adenoidectomy reduces postoperative morbidity compared to total tonsillectomy, but its role in severe pediatric OSA (AHI ≥10) has been debated due to concerns about residual disease and revision surgery rates.

METHODS

PRISMA 2020 compliant, PICO-based systematic review. 36 records identified; final cohort focused on polysomnographic improvement, residual disease (AHI normalization rates), need for additional treatment, revision/completion tonsillectomy rates, nocturnal ventilation, and postoperative complications.

RESULTS

IT achieved significant PSG improvement in severe pediatric OSA across included studies. While residual OSA rates were higher than in mild-moderate cases, revision/completion tonsillectomy rates were acceptable and complication profiles favorable. Pooled data support IT as an effective first approach even in severe pediatric OSA when adenoidectomy is combined.

LIMITATIONS

High heterogeneity in OSA severity thresholds, surgical technique definitions, and follow-up periods; most included studies are retrospective; no direct RCT comparing IT vs. total tonsillectomy specifically in severe OSA.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Intracapsular tonsillectomy with adenoidectomy is a viable approach for severe pediatric OSA, offering a better postoperative pain and complication profile without unacceptable residual disease rates. Pediatric ENT surgeons can justify IT for severe OSA rather than defaulting to total tonsillectomy, with appropriate PSG follow-up.

Why score 4: Addresses a genuinely contested question — whether intracapsular tonsillectomy is safe and effective even for severe pediatric OSA. Multi-center IRCCS-based group, PRISMA-guided, focuses on polysomnographic outcomes and re-operation rates.

Evaluation and Management of Laryngopharyngeal Reflux Disease: An Updated State of the Art Review

Lechien JR, Johnston N, Savarino E … Carroll TL (senior, Harvard Medical School, Boston, MA)

BACKGROUND

Laryngopharyngeal reflux disease (LPRD) is among the most common conditions presenting to otolaryngologists, yet its pathophysiology, optimal diagnostic workup, and treatment protocols remain debated.

METHODS

Four authors conducted a PRISMA-guided literature review of PubMed, Cochrane Library, and Scopus (last 5 years). Critical analysis yielded an evidence-based management algorithm for clinical practice.

RESULTS

New data emphasize the gaseous and alkaline profile of LPRD on 24-hour hypopharyngeal-esophageal impedance-pH monitoring, and document alkaline-activated enzymes (pepsin, bile acids) in upper aerodigestive tract mucosa. Three international consensuses inform the new algorithm: empirical PPI trial is still first-line, but impedance-pH monitoring is now prioritized for non-responders before escalating therapy. Alginate-based and dietary interventions have emerging evidence.

LIMITATIONS

Predominantly narrative review with variable-quality primary evidence; diagnostic gold standard (hypopharyngeal impedance-pH) remains underutilized due to cost and access.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For patients with LPRD not responding to an empiric PPI trial, the updated algorithm recommends hypopharyngeal-esophageal impedance-pH monitoring before further escalation. Alkaline/gaseous reflux — not captured by pH-only monitoring — may explain PPI non-response. Consider alginates and dietary alkaline modification as adjuncts.

Why score 4: Comprehensive PRISMA-guided review from a Harvard laryngology group, published in the AAO-HNS official journal. Delivers an updated management algorithm incorporating new impedance-pH monitoring data and consensus statements. High clinical utility score.

Systematic ReviewPediatric RRPn=74

Efficacy and Safety of Systemic Bevacizumab in Children with Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis: A Systematic Review

Gierlotka A, Bielecki I … Bielecki I (senior, Medical University of Silesia, Katowice, Poland)

BACKGROUND

Juvenile-onset recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (JoRRP) is driven by HPV-6/11 and causes progressive airway obstruction requiring repeated surgical debridement. Systemic bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) has emerged as an adjuvant to reduce disease burden.

METHODS

PRISMA 2020 systematic review. MEDLINE, Embase, ScienceDirect, and Cochrane Library searched from inception to June 30, 2026. Included: pediatric patients (<18 years) with JoRRP treated with intravenous bevacizumab. Outcomes: disease burden, inter-surgical interval, complete remission, and adverse events.

RESULTS

22 studies, 74 pediatric patients. Systemic bevacizumab consistently reduced disease burden, prolonged inter-surgical intervals, and decreased the frequency of operative interventions. Complete clinical remission was documented in a subset of patients. The adverse event profile was manageable, with hypertension and proteinuria as the most reported toxicities.

LIMITATIONS

Small total sample (74 patients), predominantly case series/reports; no RCT; heterogeneous dosing protocols and outcome measures; publication bias likely given favorable cases more likely reported.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For pediatric patients with aggressive JoRRP requiring frequent surgical intervention, systemic bevacizumab merits discussion as an adjuvant therapy to prolong inter-surgical intervals and reduce disease burden. Referral to a center with RRP expertise and multidisciplinary oversight (pediatric laryngology + oncology) is advisable before initiating systemic anti-VEGF therapy.

Why score 4: Juvenile-onset RRP is a challenging HPV-driven condition with no curative medical therapy. This PRISMA 2020 SR (22 studies, 74 pediatric patients) consolidates evidence on systemic bevacizumab — a treatment gaining traction as an adjunct. Practice-influencing for laryngologists managing severe JoRRP.
