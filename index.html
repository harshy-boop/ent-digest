Treatment of Locally Advanced Layngohypopharyngeal Cancers: A Network Meta-Analysis.

Capriotti V, Cin ED, De Stefani A, Nardone M et al. (+3) … Petrelli F (Oncology Unit, ASST Bergamo Ovest, Treviglio, Italy.)

BACKGROUND

Locally advanced and resectable laryngeal/hypopharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) carries high morbidity from total laryngectomy; organ-preservation strategies have proliferated but their relative efficacy has not been ranked against each other in a unified analysis.

METHODS

Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs (n = 4,395) enrolling adults with locally advanced/resectable laryngeal/hypopharyngeal SCC. Thirteen non-surgical strategies evaluated including concurrent cisplatin CTRT, induction TPF → RT, RT ± cetuximab, altered-fractionation RT, and surgery + postop RT. Outcomes: laryngeal preservation, overall survival, locoregional control.

RESULTS

For laryngeal preservation, cisplatin CTRT ranked highest (SUCRA 0.73), followed by induction TPF → RT (0.72). All non-surgical strategies outperformed surgery + postoperative RT for preservation (HR 0.34; 95% CI 0.20–0.58). For overall survival, induction PF → RT ranked best (SUCRA 0.68). For locoregional control, induction TPF → RT ranked best (SUCRA 0.94).

LIMITATIONS

Older RCTs may not reflect modern imaging, surgical technique, or systemic regimens. Heterogeneity across fractionation schedules and cisplatin dosing. No immunotherapy arms included. SUCRA rankings do not equate to statistical superiority in all pairwise comparisons.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Cisplatin-based concurrent chemoradiation remains the standard organ-preservation strategy for resectable laryngeal/hypopharyngeal SCC. Induction TPF followed by RT is the preferred option for locoregional control. Surgery + postoperative RT should be reserved for cases where preservation fails or is not feasible; offering it upfront as the preferred organ-preservation path is not supported by the data.

Why score 4: Network meta-analysis of 15 RCTs provides the highest level of comparative evidence available for this treatment decision. Published in Laryngoscope (ENT flagship), directly informs the organ-preservation vs. upfront surgery debate that arises weekly in H&N tumor boards.

Comparison of upper airway stimulation, tongue base surgeries, and continuous positive airway pressure for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea: a systematic review and network meta-analysis.

Kim DH, Jang DW, Hwang SH … Hwang SH (Department of Otolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery, Bucheon )

BACKGROUND

Moderate-to-severe OSA resistant to CPAP is increasingly managed with upper airway stimulation (UAS/Inspire) or tongue base surgery (TORS, plasma ablation). A head-to-head comparison across all three modalities in the same framework has been lacking.

METHODS

Systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis of 10 studies (n = 758) comparing UAS, TORS, plasma ablation tongue base reduction (PATBR), and CPAP. Outcomes: AHI reduction, ESS, lowest SpO₂, hospital stay, surgical success (AHI < 20 with ≥ 50% reduction), cure rate (AHI < 5).

RESULTS

UAS delivered significantly greater AHI reduction than TORS (SMD = 1.36 [0.27–2.46]) and significantly improved lowest SpO₂ vs. TORS (SMD = 1.46 [0.30–2.62]). CPAP showed greater (non-significant) AHI improvement vs. TORS (SMD = 1.17). ESS improvement did not differ significantly across arms. CPAP (OR 3.73) and UAS (OR 5.58) had significantly higher surgical success rates vs. TORS. Hospital stay was shortest for PATBR and UAS vs. TORS.

LIMITATIONS

Small total n (758) limits precision. Studies vary in patient selection — TORS patients may include those with concurrent multilevel pathology. CPAP is not a surgical option so comparison of "success rates" conflates different metrics. Short-term follow-up in several studies.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

For CPAP-intolerant patients with tongue base collapse, UAS outperforms TORS on objective AHI reduction, oxygenation, and success rates with shorter hospital stay. TORS alone as a single-level procedure for moderate-to-severe OSA has the weakest evidence among these options. DISE-guided patient selection for UAS remains essential.

Why score 4: Network meta-analysis directly addresses the most contested day-to-day decision in sleep surgery: which intervention for CPAP-intolerant patients with tongue base collapse. UAS vs. TORS is a live debate in many ENT practices; this analysis provides the most objective ranking available.

A Systematic Review of Audiological Criteria Used to Determine the Need for MRI Screening in Patients With Suspected Vestibular Schwannoma.

Awad K, Katz G, Freeman S, Lloyd S et al. (+1) … Heward E (Division of Infection, Immunity and Respiratory Medicine, Sc)

BACKGROUND

Current guidelines vary substantially on which audiometric criteria should trigger MRI referral to screen for vestibular schwannoma (VS) in patients with asymmetric SNHL. There is no universally agreed threshold.

METHODS

PRISMA-compliant systematic review (PROSPERO CRD420251131909) across Medline, PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and Scopus. 20 studies comprising 9,119 patients (1,169 VS cases) meeting inclusion criteria. Primary outcomes: sensitivity and specificity of audiometric criteria (pure tone audiometry thresholds, ABR, vestibular testing, audiometric configuration) vs. MRI.

RESULTS

PTA threshold criteria showed an inverse sensitivity-specificity tradeoff: lower thresholds (e.g., ≥15 dB asymmetry) approached 100% sensitivity but markedly reduced specificity, increasing MRI utilization. Stricter criteria improved specificity but often reduced sensitivity below the 90% threshold considered acceptable for screening. ABR demonstrated high sensitivity (≥ 90%) across most studies. Vestibular testing data were limited.

LIMITATIONS

High clinical and methodological heterogeneity precluded formal meta-analysis; narrative synthesis only. Variable VS prevalence across studies affects predictive values. Most studies retrospective with referral bias.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

No single audiometric criterion achieves both adequate sensitivity (≥ 90%) and acceptable specificity for VS screening. ABR may offer the most reliable single-test sensitivity. Practitioners who tighten MRI thresholds to reduce imaging volume risk missing small VS with preserved hearing. A risk-stratified, shared decision-making approach incorporating patient age, hearing trajectory, and tinnitus burden is more defensible than any fixed audiometric cutoff.

Why score 4: Largest systematic review on VS audiometric screening (n = 9,119), directly addresses a daily clinical decision for every otologist and general ENT who manages asymmetric SNHL. In an era of cost consciousness and MRI wait times, the "how tight can I draw the referral threshold" question is active.

Conventional clamp-and-tie hemostasis versus energy-based hemostasis devices in total thyroidectomy: a systematic review and Bayesian network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.

Awad B, Ashi MA, Abualjamal RY, Allahim MA et al. (+6) … Halawani M (King Abdullah International Medical Research Center , Jeddah)

BACKGROUND

Total thyroidectomy is performed either with conventional clamp-and-tie hemostasis (CH) or with advanced energy-based hemostasis devices (HEDs) — Harmonic Scalpel (HS) or LigaSure (LS). Evidence on optimal device choice for safety and efficiency has been mixed.

METHODS

Bayesian network meta-analysis of 22 prospective RCTs (3,824 patients) published 2005–2022. Interventions: HS, LS, CH. Primary outcomes: operative time, intraoperative blood loss, transient RLN injury, transient hypocalcemia. Secondary: postoperative hematoma, drain output, hospital stay.

RESULTS

Both HS and LS significantly reduced operative time by approximately 22 minutes vs. CH (HS SUCRA 0.76, LS SUCRA 0.73). HS further demonstrated additional significant advantages in intraoperative blood loss and drain output. Transient RLN injury and hypocalcemia rates did not significantly differ across devices. Postoperative hematoma was comparable across arms.

LIMITATIONS

Studies span 2005–2022, with variation in technique, surgeon experience, and device generations. Permanent complications (RLN injury, hypoparathyroidism) were too rare in RCTs to power meaningful comparisons. Publication bias possible for positive trials.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

Harmonic Scalpel and LigaSure both reduce total thyroidectomy operative time by ~22 minutes without increasing RLN injury or hypocalcemia risk vs. clamp-and-tie. Harmonic Scalpel edges ahead on blood loss and drain output. For high-volume thyroid practices, energy device adoption is justified on efficiency and safety grounds — and reduces anesthesia exposure at no added complication cost.

Why score 4: Bayesian network meta-analysis of 22 RCTs provides the definitive head-to-head ranking of hemostasis approaches in thyroidectomy. Operative time reduction of 22 minutes has real throughput implications; the safety equivalence data will support adoption of energy devices where budget or institutional habit has been the barrier.

Reassessing Radioactive Iodine Use After Thyroidectomy in Low-Risk Differentiated Thyroid Cancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Asif M, Nisar M, Asif A, Batool M et al. (+2) … Amin MHJ (Alzaiem Alazhari University, Khartoum, Sudan.)

BACKGROUND

American Thyroid Association guidelines recommend against routine radioactive iodine (RAI) in low-risk differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) post-thyroidectomy, yet RAI continues to be administered at high rates. Evidence on its net benefit in this population remains contested.

METHODS

Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational studies and RCTs comparing RAI vs. no RAI in low-risk DTC patients. Databases: MEDLINE, Scopus, Cochrane, Google Scholar (through December 2025). 10 studies (n = 5,260) included. Outcomes: recurrence, recurrence-free survival (5-year), treatment response, serum thyroglobulin, and side effects. Random-effects model; results as OR (95% CI).

RESULTS

No statistically significant difference between RAI and no-RAI groups for: recurrence (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.41–1.08; p = 0.10), 5-year recurrence-free survival (OR 2.47, 95% CI 0.63–9.62; p = 0.19), or excellent treatment response (OR 1.00; p = 1.00). Xerostomia was significantly more common with RAI (OR 5.x, p < 0.05).

LIMITATIONS

Predominantly observational studies with heterogeneous definitions of "low-risk." Limited long-term follow-up (most studies < 10 years). RAI dose and protocol varied across studies. Publication bias possible.

CLINICAL TAKEAWAY

RAI offers no measurable improvement in recurrence or survival for low-risk DTC, while adding salivary morbidity (xerostomia). This meta-analysis reinforces current ATA guideline recommendations against routine RAI in this population. Head and neck surgeons and endocrinologists should be confident in shared decision conversations declining RAI for low-risk patients, especially younger patients where lifetime salivary morbidity matters.

Why score 4: Meta-analysis of 10 studies (n = 5,260) addresses a genuine practice controversy — despite guidelines, RAI overuse in low-risk thyroid cancer remains common. The null efficacy + xerostomia findings provide strong ammunition for guideline adherence conversations. Directly affects post-thyroidectomy care delivered by ENT/H&N surgeons.
